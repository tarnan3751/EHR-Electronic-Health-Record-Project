# UC 23 — Draft a note from a recorded visit

SwiftChart EHR · the expansion plan's step 16 (proposed FR-62) · Tahseen Arnan · a proposed use case, not in the course document

A clinician records a visit's conversation, with the agreement of everyone who will speak, and SwiftChart drafts the
note's Subjective, Objective, Assessment and Plan from it into the same review as a dictation (UC 13): nothing goes into
the note until the clinician has accepted, edited or rejected each proposal. It works on the clinic's own app only, in
English only, and keeps the conversation no longer than it must. All data in its tests is synthetic, and the eval's
voices are made by a text-to-speech model: no real person's voice is ever recorded.

**It ships switched off** (`Ehr:Ambient:Enabled`, false in `src/Ehr.Web/appsettings.json`; on in the local stack, whose
data is synthetic). The drafts it gives are thin and mostly warned on: recording a patient's visit for so little is a
cost without the benefit. Switching it on is the clinic owner's decision, after reading what the last run measured.

**What the last run measured** (2026-11-24, on the developer Mac, not the clinic's server: the eval's 22 synthetic
recordings of 12 visits, 12 clean, 6 in a room and 4 with babble; `eval/results/ambient-latest.json`, its stored answers
scored again with the fix round's checks; `docs/expansion/PLAN.md`, step 16):

- *The draft is thin.* Field F1 against the rule-following gold 0.3155, Subjective's 0.174; 40 values offered across the
  22 recordings, 69 of their 133 sources heard differently from what was said. As the run first scored them, F1 was
  0.3558 and 41 values were offered, but seven sources offered a question's words as a finding, one of those quoted
  the clinician in Subjective without a warning, and one Plan carried an order to the pharmacy. The checks now refuse
  the four values the seven sat in, and one more that offered the clinician's words as the patient's, which is what
  lowered F1; the Plan is still offered, the order cut from it and listed for the clinician to place.
- *What the checks can and cannot promise.* "No order shown" and "no question offered as a finding" are true of this
  run's drafts only. The checks find an order or a question only in the forms they read, and speech recognition hears
  both in others: "i'll get that sent over to the chemistvi", "i'll pop it over to the chemistvi", "it'll be waiting
  for you at the chemistvi", "i'll book you in for an altrasound" and "add penicillin to your alergy list" all get
  through, as do a "no" heard as "know" (twice in one of this run's transcripts), "ill need an injection", and a
  clinician's question pulled into the patient's voice and answered without an answer word. The rescore before this
  run also reached zero, and this run found nine. Speech recognition wrote no question mark in any of the 22
  transcripts, and 212 of the 554 questions the visits' scripts ask are found by none of the checks' rules, 27 of them
  with no word heard at all (counted word by word: a question is found when a word it was heard as lies inside a stretch
  the checks take for a question; `metrics.questions` in the results).
- *Who spoke.* 0.5882 of the words labelled with the right speaker and 0.349 Unsure; 65 of 22,225 words labelled another
  speaker's with confidence, in 18 passages (1 word in a clean recording, 6 in a room, 58 in babble). Two clean visits
  were more than half Unsure (c06 0.6328, c12 0.5006). Swept one at a time: the margin from 0 to 0.3 keeps the words
  labelled right between 0.5692 and 0.5882 and the passages labelled wrong between 16 and 18; the floor from 0.3 to 0.7
  takes them from 0.7667 right and 55 passages wrong to 0.0843 right and 1 wrong (0.5 in use); the drop rule, off in
  use, from 0.05 to 0.3, from 0.1031 right and 9 wrong to 0.5882 right and 18 wrong.
- *Speech.* The patient's word error rate on the clean recordings 0.0752, under its limit of twice dictation's (0.096).
- *Time.* Every visit drafted: its parts took 25.18 seconds on average and 55.25 at most (the limit is 60), 2 were
  drafted again as halves, and the longest visit took 199.2 seconds to draft (the limit is 8 minutes). While listening,
  each five-second chunk answered in 0.768 seconds at the 95th percentile and 1.15 at most near the end of the
  14.3-minute visit. A dictation sorted beside a visit being drafted took its usual time: 50.4 seconds against 52.0
  alone, two at once 70.0 each against 75.1.

Off, nothing offers it, the Listen page and every route of its API answer 404, and no consent can be written; the
reviews of visits recorded while it was on still open and can be finished, and their transcripts are still deleted on
time. The Listen page's 404 says "Listening to a visit is switched off at this clinic." with the way back to the chart
to a clinician whose patient it is, and reads nothing about the patient; anyone else gets the 404 any patient's address
they can't see answers, the same whether the patient exists or not.

## Clinician workflow

- **Ask first.** From the chart (**Listen to the visit**, beside **Dictate a note**) or a draft (**Listen to the visit
  into this draft**), the page opens on its consent. It gives the statement to read aloud: "I'd like SwiftChart to listen
  to our conversation to help me write today's note. The recording is deleted as soon as it's turned into text. I check
  everything before it goes in your record. The text is deleted once I've checked it, or after a week at the latest,
  except what it suggested for the note. You can say no, or ask me to stop at any time, and your care won't change
  either way. Is that all right with you?" Beneath it, for the clinician and not read aloud: for a child, ask a parent
  or guardian, and the child too if they're old enough to understand; for anyone else who can't agree for themselves,
  the person who decides for them; someone the patient shares their portal record with can't agree in their place; not
  for a visit in another language or through an interpreter; the clinic's backups keep the database, this text
  included, for up to 7 days. The clinician says who will speak (the patient
  and me, or the patient, me and one other person) and who agreed (the patient, or someone who decides for them), and
  ticks "Everyone who will speak has agreed, aloud, to this visit being recorded to draft the note." **Start listening**
  records the consent on the server, and only then does the page ask for the microphone. Nothing is captured or kept in
  the browser before.
- **While listening.** A line under the heading says "Recording the visit: 3:12 of 20:00." with its red dot, and the
  tab's title starts "● Recording:"; that clock isn't announced to a screen reader every second. The status line, which
  is, says each change (listening, paused, listening again) and each warning, and keeps it until the next. No transcript
  is shown in the room. **Pause listening** pauses the recorder and switches the microphone's tracks off until **Resume
  listening**; beside it: "If anyone else comes in, pause until they have agreed or left." The page pauses itself the
  same way when it goes out of sight, even while listening is starting, and waits for the clinician to resume.
- **It stops on its own** at 20 minutes, or after 5 minutes in which nobody has spoken, and drafts what it has. The
  status line warns once before each, in warning style: "Listening stops in a minute, at its 20-minute limit.", and
  "Nobody has spoken for 4 minutes: listening stops in a minute unless someone speaks."; at the limit it says so once
  with what happens next, the voices named and the note drafted. If the clinician is signed out, or the listening is
  refused, it stops at once and keeps nothing.
- **Stop and discard** at any point before the note is drafted, or leaving the page then, switches the microphone off
  first, then discards the session at the dictation service and its review. Nothing recorded is kept, and the cursor
  goes back to the consent. This is what to do if the patient changes their mind. Leaving the page at any time ends
  listening there for good; coming back to it says so. Once the voices have been named and the service has taken them,
  leaving the page keeps the visit: its draft is retrieved from the note's draft.
- **When it can't be fetched or drafted** the page never stops at a dead end. Before the voices are named, if the
  service doesn't answer, it offers **Try again** and **Discard the recording**; if the visit came back unsorted, or with
  more voices than can be named (9), only **Discard the recording**. Once they have been named, **Try again** and
  **Open the draft**, where Retrieve stages it. A visit left behind (the page closed, or not drafted) says so on its
  review, opened from the draft: "A visit recorded at 2026-11-22 14:02 CST hasn't been drafted yet. If its voices were
  named a moment ago, wait a minute and choose Retrieve. Otherwise discard it to dictate or listen again.", with
  **Retrieve the visit's draft** first and **Discard the visit** after it; retrieving one whose voices were never named
  is refused with why. A visit the dictation service was too busy to draft is staged with its transcript and "The
  dictation service was busy for too long to draft this visit. The conversation is kept with this review until you
  finish it: write the note from it, or discard it."
- **Who is speaking.** After **Stop and draft the note**, the visit is transcribed and its voices told apart. Each voice
  is listed with its first words and how long it spoke, and a "Choose who it is" select with nothing chosen: **Draft the
  note** stays disabled until every voice has a role, one of them the clinician and one the patient, and says so
  under it ("Name every voice to draft the note."). Beyond those two, a
  voice can be someone else in the room, once, if three were to speak, or "I can't tell", for any number: telling voices
  apart can split one person in two or hand over a fragment. With only the patient and the clinician to speak, any
  other voice can only be "I can't tell", and the page says so; its words are shown as "Unsure" in the review. The note's
  Subjective comes only from what the patient or someone with them said; its Objective, Assessment and Plan only from
  what the clinician said.
- **Review.** The draft goes to the review page, as a dictation's does, with the transcript a turn at a time, each led by
  who said it. Each proposal points at the words it came from. A Subjective value is the patient's own words, in the
  first person, and says so: "In the patient's own words. Reword it as your note before you accept it." Others may say
  who said it couldn't be told with confidence, that someone else in the room said it, or that part of the visit had
  something for that section which didn't pass the checks. Nothing is proposed for orders or the allergy list: those
  the clinician said are listed as not carried over, as a dictation's are, and one said where who spoke couldn't be told
  says "Said in a passage whose speaker couldn't be told." beside its words; a patient's request is never listed.
- **Finishing** the review deletes the conversation's transcript. "Reject every proposal and delete the conversation"
  does the same without adding anything to the note: for a patient who withdraws their consent after the visit.
- **Off-site** the chart offers no listening, and the page says "Unavailable: listening to a visit works only on the
  clinic's network." A review of a visit whose transcript is still kept can't be decided or finished off-site: the cloud
  site shows the proposals and "Finish this review at the clinic: the conversation is read only on the clinic's network".

## What is kept, where and for how long

| What | Where | How long |
|---|---|---|
| Audio, as recorded | The browser's memory, waiting to be sent | Until sent, discarded or the page closes |
| Audio | The dictation service's memory, on the clinic's server | Until it is transcribed, discarded, or 10 idle minutes: never on disk or in a log |
| Who spoke when | The service's memory, while it is worked out | Dropped once the voices are told apart: no voiceprint is kept |
| Transcript and draft | The service's memory | Deleted as soon as the review is saved; otherwise 10 minutes after it is ready |
| The conversation's transcript | `ehr.ambient_transcripts` on the clinic's database (and the cloud standby's storage, which the cloud app can't read) | Until the review is finished, or 7 days after it was written if nobody has finished it. The backup host keeps it with everything else, for 7 days at most after it is deleted (a base backup every 23 hours and a 6-day recovery window, `infra/barman`, as `infra/compose/README.md` works out) |
| The consent | `ehr.ambient_consents`, and its write in the audit log | For good: who, which patient, when, how many people would speak and who agreed. No names, no text |
| The proposals and decisions | `ehr.ai_note_reviews` (no transcript text) and `ehr.ai_field_proposals` | For good, as a dictation's are, as sensitive items: administrators never see their values |
| The audit log's copy of the transcript | `ehr.audit_events` | Never: its SHA-256 and length only |

## Decisions

- **Consent is a row the database checks.** `ehr.ambient_consents` is written before any audio, as the signed-in
  clinician, for a patient on their panel, at the database's time, and never changes; its write is the consent's record
  in the audit log. A review of a conversation names it, and the database refuses one without a consent of its own, given
  by its clinician for its patient at most 10 minutes before (`db/policies/56-ambient-listening.sql`). Every chunk of
  audio passes the same check in the app before it goes to the service, and the service keeps a visit's sessions apart
  from dictations', as the API does: a dictation's start never hands back a visit's review. A start that fails once the
  service has begun its session, refused or not, discards that session at once.
- **The conversation's transcript is kept apart and only while it's needed.** The review's own record keeps no transcript
  text: the database holds its stored result to where each passage was and who said it (the app keeps none of the
  transcript's segments there, only each proposal's sources), the contract's codes (an empty
  field's reasons the service's own), the service's short code-shaped names for its models, and the app's own sentence
  for a result it couldn't sort; the one place left for words is the value each proposal offers
  (`ehr.ambient_result_is_bare`). The transcript is written once, as the review is staged, deleted in the transaction that finishes it
  (the database refuses a finished review that still has one), and after 7 days at most by the on-site app's hourly
  timer, as the system's act, through `ehr.purge_ambient_transcripts`. After that, the review's proposals can be
  rejected, or edited into the clinician's own words, but not accepted, nor edited back to the words offered (the box
  to edit one starts empty, and says why): nobody can check them against what was said. An edit counts as the words
  offered when it reads the same with case, punctuation, spacing and the characters that show nothing ignored, and
  compatibility forms folded (`ehr.ambient_words`, and the app's `OfferedWords`, the same): a speed bump against putting
  unchecked words in unchanged, not proof that the clinician rewrote them. A look-alike letter of another alphabet, or
  one word changed, gets past it.
- **The clinician says who each voice is**, every one, with none chosen for them: a wrong default would be a whole
  visit's words filed under the wrong speaker. One clinician, one patient, at most one other, and "I can't tell" for any
  voice beyond those, whose passages are then Unsure. The API and the dictation service refuse a mapping that leaves a voice out,
  and the API one that names more people, all but "I can't tell", than the consent said would speak. A draft that doesn't say who said each
  passage, sorted or not, or whose Subjective value doesn't say it is the patient's own words, isn't staged.
- **Decided at the clinic.** While a review's transcript is kept, its proposals are decided, and it is finished, at the
  clinic only: the cloud site shows them without the transcript, and the clinic's server refuses a decision, finishing
  or rejecting everything that the cloud app forwards. Anything carrying the cloud app's forwarding header counts as
  off-site whether or not its proof checks out, so the rule holds if the two apps' shared key is missing or differs; the
  same goes for offering listening on the chart, the draft and the Listen page.

## Limits

- The dictation service tells the voices apart with a speaker-embedding model trained on VoxCeleb
  (`speechbrain/spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb`, Apache 2.0), clustered into as many voices as the clinician said would speak. A
  visit in which fewer than two voices said anything (a few seconds of speech) can't have its voices named, and can
  only be discarded. The web app's tests use a stub of the service, which answers as the service does (its answers are
  pinned in `src/Ehr.Dictation/contract`).
- A visit's note is drafted a part of about 450 tokens at a time, each answer at most 1,100 tokens, so it ends within
  about a minute; a part that times out or runs out of tokens is drafted once more as two halves. If K2 Horizon still
  can't draft one part, the whole visit comes back undrafted, with its transcript, rather than a note with a part
  missing. Dictations come first: a part being drafted gives way the moment a dictation asks, and a visit whose parts
  have waited ten minutes in all comes back undrafted, with its transcript, saying the server was too busy.
- A passage is Unsure when the service can't say whose it is with confidence: it has fewer than four words, its windows
  sit close to two voices or far from their own voice's centre, the smoothing or a short turn joining its neighbour
  moved one of them, they came from a group too small to be a voice, or its voice was named "I can't tell". So a short
  answer between two of the clinician's questions is Unsure rather than the clinician's. An Unsure source is offered
  with a warning, but Subjective never takes one in the clinician's voice: an Unsure passage is nearly always said by
  the voice it was heard in, and those the drafts quoted for Subjective were mostly the clinician's own words, often a
  question. An instruction said in an Unsure passage is listed as not carried over, marked as such. The
  thresholds are the values the eval's second visits run chose, which its visits go on measuring.
- Speech recognition hears conversation without punctuation, so a negation or a hedge reaches the words after it until
  a new clause or the next speaker, and the checks throw away more of a conversation than of a dictation; the clinician
  writes the rest. A hedge, a denial or a report reaches past what people put before what it is about, fillers, a verb
  of belief and a subject with its verb: "I think um you have gout", "I don't reckon I've had a fever", "it's unlikely
  you've had a stroke" and "my wife thinks I have sleep apnoea" are never offered without their hedge or report.
- A question the checks find is never a finding's source, whoever asks it: by its words ("have you had any fever",
  "any chest pain", "do I need antibiotics", a sentence opening with "will" or "can"), or because the next speaker
  answers it, an answer heard with a letter lost ("ys please") included. An answer alone ("not really") is never a
  value. Speech recognition writes no question mark and often loses a question's first word, so a question asked in
  other words, or heard without its first word, may still be taken for a statement and offered as a finding: in the
  last run 212 of the 554 questions the scripts ask were found by none of the rules, counted word by word as above,
  though none was offered.
- An instruction said to the patient is cut and listed when it is talk of an ordering act or of the prescription itself,
  found as a noun of the prescription, a supply, a test or referral, or the allergy list with its verb in one clause
  ("I'll send that to your pharmacy", "I'll pop a form in for some blood tests", "I'll put you down as allergic to
  penicillin"), or, as the clinician's own act, a prescription sent on, by its frame, whatever the place was heard as
  ("I'll send it over to the chemist", heard as "…the chemistvi"); care described without one stays in the Plan
  ("we'll get some bloods today", "I'll refer you to the physio"), and so does a test spoken of only as a possibility
  ("if it doesn't settle we can arrange an ultrasound").
  Where that line falls needs a clinician's reading.
- Speakers are told apart well enough to label each passage, not overlapping speech, which gets the louder voice's label;
  and the voices in the tests are synthetic. How it does on real voices is unknown until real recordings are allowed.
- The browser tests run in Chromium: Safari's long recordings and its pausing aren't covered by them.
- The consent statement, the guidance on children, representatives and proxies, and the 5 minutes of quiet need a
  clinician's reading, and probably a legal one: whether recording needs every speaker's agreement is a matter of local
  law.
