# UC 11 — Sign in with multi-factor authentication

SwiftChart EHR · Luke Garcia · CS 3354 Software Engineering, Fall 2026

This file records what the expansion plan (`docs/expansion/PLAN.md`) has changed in this use case. The use case itself, and its first six test cases (`tests/Ehr.Web.Tests/TestCases/UC11`), are Luke's.

## A link opens its page alone after sign-in (the plan's step 13b)

Someone who follows a link while signed out is sent to sign in and then on to the page the link named. Before step 13b they arrived at the link's whole address, so whoever wrote the link chose what the page did on arrival: `?sensitive=true` on a chart showed the patient's sensitive items, recorded as the clinician's own view, and a link to the portal's record file downloaded it.

- **The rule.** After a staff sign-in the app goes to the page the link named with its query removed (`Links.LocalPage` in `src/Ehr.Web/Accounts/Links.cs`). It applies at every point the staff sign-in follows a return address: after the authenticator or recovery code (`Code.cshtml.cs`), on **Continue** after a first enrolment (`Enroll.cshtml.cs`), and when someone already signed in opens the sign-in page (`SignIn.cshtml.cs`). The password step only passes the address along. The portal's sign-in follows the same rule and keeps nothing.
- **What is kept.** A patient search's own criteria, `name`, `birthdate` and `mrn` (`SearchQuery.Parameters`), so a clinician whose session ended on a search comes back to it. Everything else is dropped, `sensitive` and page handlers included. On any page but the search those three only fill the "Back to search" link.
- **Still a local address.** The address is checked as a local one first, as before; anything else goes to the home page.
- **What this leaves open.** A link can still have a clinician run a patient search on arrival: up to 50 patients are listed by name, MRN and date of birth, and each is recorded as that clinician's read. Nothing is shown that the clinician couldn't search for, but the reads are ones they didn't choose, and the portal's "Who viewed my record" (step 13c) shows them to those patients. It waits for a decision: filling the form in without running the search would close it, and changes what `LoggingTests` expects.
- **Both sign-ins on one host.** A browser that holds a portal session and signs in to the staff app through a link to the portal's record file lands on the portal's download page, not on the file.

Human review: the three redirects above and `Links.cs` decide where an authenticated session lands, so they are listed with the authorization changes in the phase 4 pull request draft.

## Sessions end when idle, and after 12 hours (the plan's step 18)

A web sign-in, a staff member's or a patient's, on the clinic's site or the cloud copy, ends 15 minutes after the last
request made with it, and 12 hours after it began however active it is (`src/Ehr.Domain/Accounts/SessionTimes.cs`). The
desktop app's session token was not changed by this step; since step 19a it works for 12 hours too (below).

- **Renewed by use.** Any request made with the sign-in cookie renews it once a minute has passed since it was last
  issued, so the end is 15 minutes after the last request, to within a minute. Before step 18 the cookie was renewed
  only once half its life had gone, so an idle staff session ended anywhere between 15 and 30 minutes, a portal one
  between 10 and 20. The account is still checked every 5 minutes however often the cookie is renewed (the check's
  time is kept in the cookie), so a disabled account loses even a busy session within 5 minutes.
- **A question renews nothing.** The page asks how long is left at `/session` (staff) or `/portal/session` (portal),
  which answer `{left, final, account, acting}` in seconds and never cached. With the `Ehr-Session-Check` header the
  request is only a question and renews nothing, at any age; the account is still checked on it, so a disabled account
  is refused. Without it, it is a keep-alive. Both pages are in their area, under its policy: an ended session is sent to
  sign in, which is how the page learns it has ended. The cloud app answers both itself, even with the clinic's server
  cut off.
- **12 hours.** `CookieSecurityStamp` ends a sign-in 12 hours after it began, on every request; a sign-in from before
  step 18 counts from its first request since, and takes the 15 idle minutes and an account check then. In its last 15
  minutes the session page says the end is final.
- **No recording cut off.** Starting a dictation (`/api/dictation/sessions`, `/for-note`) or a visit
  (`/ambient/start`) is refused with 409 `sign_in_ending`, "Sign in again first: a sign-in lasts at most 12 hours, and
  this one ends in … minutes, too soon for a …", when less is left of the 12 hours than its longest recording (3 minutes
  for a dictation, 20 for a visit, as the dictation service's pinned start answers say), as Tahseen agreed on 24
  November 2026. A dictation in Spanish (step 17) records 2 minutes at most and is held to the same 3.
- **Unchanged:** the 10 minutes between the password and the code; the area policies; the APIs' 401 where a page is
  sent to sign in.

### On the page (`src/Ehr.Design/wwwroot/js/session.js`)

Every signed-in page, staff or portal, carries a warning and what replaces the page when its session ends (the layout,
`Pages/Shared/_Layout.cshtml`). The script only asks the server; it never ends anything on the device's clock while the
server can be asked. The page carries nothing of the session's own, no account, record or time left, so two requests
for a page give the same markup: the script asks the session page for them, with a check, as the page loads.

- **Activity.** A key, a click or tap, the wheel, or typing sends a keep-alive, at most once a minute. Mouse movement
  doesn't count. Any other request, a save or a dictation's audio, renews the session on the server by itself.
- **A recording counts as activity** (Tahseen's ruling of 24 November 2026). While a dictation or a visit is being
  recorded, sent, transcribed or drafted, the page says so (`data-session-recording="recording"` on `<body>`, set by
  `dictation.js` and `ambient.js`), and the script then never opens the warning or ends the page, and sends a
  keep-alive every minute itself, so a 20-minute visit with no key pressed is never signed out. A visit paused, or
  waiting for its voices to be named, is `waiting`: it may be warned, and the warning says that the dictation service
  discards a visit left like that for 10 minutes, whether or not the person stays signed in. That is the service's own
  rule (step 16), which this step doesn't change: the warning comes at 13 idle minutes at the earliest, so a visit
  paused that long is already gone at the service, and staying signed in can't keep it.
- **The warning.** When the server confirms 2 minutes are left, a modal dialog takes the focus: "You'll be signed out
  soon", with when, "Stay signed in" and "Sign out now", and, when something was typed on the page, that it stays if
  the person is signed out. "Stay signed in" sends a keep-alive and closes the dialog; the page isn't reloaded, so what
  was typed and where the page was scrolled stay as they were. Escape does the same. If the server can't be reached,
  the dialog stays open and says so, to try again. At the 12-hour end the dialog says that a sign-in lasts at most 12
  hours, and offers "Keep working" instead, since nothing moves that end; once closed, with it or Escape, it isn't
  shown again for that sign-in, and the page ends at the 12-hour end as any other.
- **The end.** Once the server says the session is over, the page is taken off the screen, title and all, and replaced
  by "Signed out:", with why, and "Sign in again". If anything was typed on the page, or a recording is in it, the page
  is kept in the tab's memory, out of sight, never in browser storage. Signing in again, in a frame on the same page or
  in another tab of the browser, brings it back exactly as it was for the same account, and then the page's own forms
  post as before. A proxy's page goes back to the record it was about: signing in starts every portal session on the
  account's own record, so the page chooses the same grant again first, and if that grant has ended, nothing kept is
  shown. Anyone else signing in clears what was kept and goes to their own home page. A page with nothing typed is
  reloaded instead, a new recorded read.
- **Signing out on purpose** with something typed keeps it the same way. Signing out in one tab ends the others.
- **Without JavaScript** the server's end still applies, with no warning, and a form sent after it goes to sign in.
- **Never cached.** Every page served to someone signed in, on either site, is `no-store` (`NoStoreFilter`), so neither
  the browser nor anything between keeps a copy, and Back after the end, or after signing out, can't show the page
  again. A full page already was, through its sign-out form's token; the fragments a page asks for as someone types
  (the patient search's results, the code search) were not. Static files stay cacheable.

**What happens to what was typed.** Note drafts autosave as they are typed, so a note is on the server whatever the
session does (`TC-UC11-11` proves it at a timeout). Every other form keeps what was typed in the tab until the same
account signs in again:

| Page | What can be typed |
|---|---|
| Patient search (`/patients`) | A name, date of birth, MRN |
| A chart's allergy, medication or diagnosis (`/patients/{id}/allergies`, `medications`, `diagnoses`) | The entry, its reaction, its dates |
| New and discontinued orders (`/patients/{id}/orders/new`, `…/discontinue`) | The order, a reason to override, a reason to discontinue |
| Results (`/patients/{id}/orders/{order}/results`) | The values filed by hand |
| Vital signs (`/patients/{id}/vitals`, `…/vitals/error`) | The readings, a reason they were in error |
| Emergency access (`/patients/{id}/reason`) | The reason |
| Messages (`/patients/{id}/messages/new`, a conversation) | A subject and a message, a reply |
| Notes: the editor, signing, an addendum, sensitivity, a visit summary and its withdrawal | Sections (also saved on the server), reasons, an addendum, a summary |
| Dictate a note (`/patients/{id}/dictate`) | The dictation's language (step 17), kept with the page, never stored |
| A dictation's review (`…/review/{review}`) | An edited field |
| Listen to the visit (`/patients/{id}/listen`) | The consent's choices, who each voice is |
| Pre-visit forms (`/patients/{id}/forms`) | The questionnaire to ask for |
| Proxy access, clinic side (`/patients/{id}/proxies`) | An address, a reason |
| Refill review (`/patients/{id}/refills/{request}`) | A message declining it |
| Schedule (`/schedule`, booking, an appointment) | A day, a time, a reason, a reason to cancel |
| Quick text (`/quick-texts`) | A phrase and its shortcut |
| Admin: staff, audit, an emergency access to review | A new account, the audit's filters, a review's outcome and note |
| Portal: messages, a conversation, refill requests, access, a pre-visit form | A message, a reply, a note, an address, the answers |

It is lost only when the tab is closed, when someone else signs in on the browser (which clears it on purpose), when the
browser has JavaScript off, or for a proxy whose access ended while they were signed out (the text was about a record
they may no longer see). The pages written without the layout (the cloud app's "Not saved" page, the error pages) have
no warning and aren't cleared; the session still ends on the server. The desktop app was not changed by this step: its
token lasted 30 days, and it relied on the computer's own screen lock until step 19a (below).

Not covered by the browser tests, which run in Chromium: Safari's and Edge's own handling of the dialog, of timers in a
tab out of sight, and of the back-forward cache. WebKit is unverified.

## The desktop app's sign-in lasts 12 hours (the plan's step 19a)

The desktop app's token works for 12 hours after the sign-in with both factors that made it, as a web sign-in does
(Tahseen's ruling 11b of 25 November 2026, proposed FR-68); its 30 days are now only how long the app may open its
offline copy without a sign-in. After the 12 hours the server refuses it with a 401 that says the sign-in has ended
(`Ehr-Sign-In: ended`), and the app asks for both factors again. `docs/UC8.md` has what that means for sync, and
TC-UC8-07 tests it. The app's window now locks after the web's idle time, with the same warning, and opens with the
password alone within those 12 hours, checked by the server as the sign-in's password step is, with its lockout;
`docs/UC7.md` has the lock.

`LayoutTests` checks that every signed-in page, and no signed-out one, carries the warning, and `NoStoreTests` that
signed-in pages are never stored while static files are. `SessionTimeTests.cs` checks
the rest against the server alone: renewal once a minute, a check never renewing, the
same end for a patient and on the cloud app, the pending step unchanged, a busy session of a disabled account ended
at the next account check, a cookie from before step 18 given the idle time and an account check at once, and the
recordings' lengths against the dictation service's.
