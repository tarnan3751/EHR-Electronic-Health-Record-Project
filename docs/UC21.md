# UC 21 — Answer a refill request

SwiftChart EHR · the expansion plan's step 13d (proposed FR-58) · Tahseen Arnan · a proposed use case, not in the course document

## Clinician workflow

- **A patient asks.** When a patient, or someone they share their record with, asks from the portal to renew a medication, the request appears in the inbox of every clinician who has the patient on their panel and whose account an admin hasn't disabled (one locked out for a while after failed sign-ins counts): their care team. The item says only "A patient asked to renew a medication", with the patient's name and **Review the request**. It has no **Mark done**: it is done by answering.
- **Review.** The request's page (`/patients/{id}/refills/{request}`) shows the patient's banner with their allergies, the medication as it was asked for and as the chart has it now (with how it is taken and its status), who asked and when, and the patient's note. It opens on **Approve: write the order**, or, when a decline came back refused, in the message.
- **Approve.** **Approve: write the order** opens the order form (`/patients/{id}/orders/new?type=medication&refill={request}`) with the request beside it and the item fixed to the medication's own product, the catalog's item under the medication's drug code: "This order renews the same medication, lisinopril 10 MG Oral Tablet." It is the same form as any medication order: the duplicate check needs a reason for the same product already on order, under the clinic's own item for it or RxNorm's (FR-05), the safety checks show what they find and a serious warning needs a reason to go ahead (FR-47), and both reasons are kept on the order. **Sign order** signs the order and approves the request with it, in one transaction, and goes back to the request's page: "Approved:". The order is for exactly that product: another strength, form, salt or route is a new order, not a renewal, and the form refuses it, as does the database. To change the medication, decline the request with a message that says so, and write the new order from the chart.
- **Decline.** Type a message for the patient (up to 1,000 characters) and choose **Decline request**: "Declined:". The patient reads the message in the portal, under the request.
- **Either way** the item is done for the whole care team, and the patient is emailed that there is something new in their portal, with nothing about it.
- **The care team changes.** A clinician who puts the patient on their panel gets an item for each request still waiting; one who takes the patient off theirs has their items done. A request left with nobody on the panel waits, and goes to whoever joins next; the patient's page shows it waiting all along.
- **A medication stopped or entered in error** since the request, changed on the chart since it was asked ("the medication on the chart has changed since this was asked"), or no longer coded as a product the clinic orders, can't be renewed: the request's page says "Can't be approved:" and offers only **Decline**, so the patient is told why. The request stays in the care team's inboxes until someone does.

The patient's side is in `docs/UC6.md`.

## Decisions

- **Data.** `ehr.refill_requests`: the patient, the medication on the chart and its name when asked, the note, the portal account that asked and when, the state (FHIR Task's `requested`, `completed` and `rejected`, shown as waiting, approved and declined), the order that approved it and what was ordered, or the message that declined it, who answered and when, and whether it is sensitive. Patient data: row-level security is enabled and forced, and the write-audit trigger records every write. Never edited or deleted.
- **Who answers.** A clinician with the patient on their active panel, as themselves; the page asks for the panel before it reads anything, and sends anyone else to the chart, which asks for a reason when the patient isn't on theirs. Clinicians can read every request in the database, as they read charts.
- **Once.** The database refuses a second answer, from anyone: two clinicians answering at once give one answer, and the other is told "someone answered this request first", with nothing saved. A clinician who opened the order form while a colleague declined signs nothing: the order goes with the refusal.
- **What can be renewed.** A medication coded as a product the clinic orders: the order catalog has an item under the medication's own drug code. One coded by its ingredient alone, or with a code the catalog doesn't hold, has no product to renew; the portal doesn't offer it and says to call the clinic, and the database refuses a request for it.
- **An approval is a new order for the same medication product.** The database holds the request's answer to an active medication order of the same patient, signed in the same transaction by the clinician answering, for the catalog item whose code is the one the medication had when it was asked, which the database copies onto the request and which the chart's entry must still have: a request whose medication a clinician changed since (`65-chart-lists.sql`) is only declined. One order for one request, while the medication is still active, and never an order that decides a dictation's proposal, nor does one that answers a request decide a proposal (the expansion plan's step 15; `55-ai-review.sql` holds the other way): approving takes the order's lock after the request's row, and deciding a proposal takes it too, so of the two at once the second waits and then sees the first. Another strength, form, salt or route is refused, as is the clinic's own item for the same drug; with that item already on order, the approval is a duplicate and needs the duplicate's reason, as any order does (`60-orders.sql`). Approving locks the medication, then the request, the order in which marking the medication sensitive takes them, so the two wait for each other and never deadlock; stopping the medication waits for the approval, or the approval sees it stopped. An order signed in an earlier transaction can't close a request. The harmless case it allows: an order the same clinician signed, for the same patient and medication, in another transaction that began after this one and committed before this one answered.
- **Nothing is bypassed or signed by itself.** Approving is the order form, every check of an order with it; the order is listed on the chart, audited and discontinued like any other. A request names the order that approved it; the order is unchanged.
- **The `refill` in the order form's address is never needed across a sign-in.** The staff sign-in goes on to the page a link named without its query, so after one the clinician is on a new order with no request: approving is done again from the request's page.
- **The inbox.** A trigger that runs as the tables' owner puts the request in the care team's inboxes and marks every item done when the request is answered; no runtime role can insert an item or mark this kind done. A request nobody could be told of is refused. Two more, on the panel (`ehr.patient_assignments`), keep the items with the care team: a clinician who joins gets an item for each waiting request, never a second one; one who leaves has theirs done. Locked out for a while after failed sign-ins counts as on the care team, disabled by an admin doesn't, as for appointments (`80-appointments.sql`). Asking, answering, joining and leaving each take one lock for the patient before they read, so two at once see each other: a clinician who joins while a request is asked gets it, one who joins while it is answered is left no item open, and one who leaves while it is asked is given none.
- **Sensitive.** A request is sensitive when its medication is, and follows it when the medication is marked or unmarked. Its page then records a view of the patient's sensitive items, as the order form for it does.
- **Email.** At most one a day for answers to refill requests: the first answer of the clinic's day, from midnight in its time zone, sends it. To the patient's own confirmed account, naming nothing, with a link to the portal's home. Never to a proxy.
- **Admins never see a request's values** in the audit log: not the medication, the note or the message (`ehr.shown_audit_details` leaves the table out).
- **Reads.** The request's page is one recorded read of the patient (a view of sensitive items when the request is sensitive); HEAD is refused before anything is read. A decline refused (no message, or too long) shows the page again, read again: a second recorded read, which is what happened.
- **Off-site.** The request is read from the cloud copy; a decline, and the order form's post, are forwarded to the clinic's server. While that can't be reached the answers are replaced by "Answering a refill request needs the clinic's server.", and the "Not saved" page shows a message typed to decline.
- **The decline message is not checked for internal codes,** as a visit summary is (step 13a): it is the clinician's own words to the patient, as a step 5 message is.

## Limits

- Nothing is sent to a pharmacy; the order is the clinic's record of the prescription.
- Approving doesn't change the chart's medication list, nor does a new order written after a decline: a change of medication is written there by hand.
- Renewing at another strength or form takes two steps, a decline with a message and a new order from the chart: an approval renews the same product only.
- The order form doesn't fill in the dose, route and frequency: the chart's dosage is free text, shown beside the form.
- A patient can't withdraw a request; the care team answers every one.
- The chart doesn't show requests; the inbox is where they are answered.
- Requests aren't in the desktop app, the FHIR API or the downloaded record.
