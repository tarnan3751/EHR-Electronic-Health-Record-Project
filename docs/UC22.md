# UC 22 — Answer a pre-visit form

SwiftChart EHR · the expansion plan's step 13e (proposed FR-59) · Tahseen Arnan · a proposed use case, not in the course document

A clinician asks a patient to fill in a questionnaire before a visit; the patient, or someone they share their record with and allowed sensitive items, fills it in in the portal; the clinic's database scores the answers, and the clinician reads them, for that visit and over time. The PHQ-9 (depression) and the GAD-7 (anxiety) are built in, worded exactly as their authors publish them (`docs/expansion/reference-data.md`, "Pre-visit questionnaires"). Every form, its request, its answers and its score, is a sensitive item.

## Patient workflow

- **Find it.** The portal's home always links to **Pre-visit forms**, for the patient and for a proxy allowed sensitive items, whether or not the record has any forms, so the link says nothing of them; while one can be filled in, it reads **Pre-visit forms (1 to fill in)**. **Pre-visit forms** (`/portal/forms`) lists those to fill in, soonest first, each with its appointment and when it opens and closes ("Fill it in by …", or "You can fill it in from … until …", in the portal's date format, labeled UTC), and those sent. A form that closed unanswered is no longer listed.
- **Fill it in.** **Fill in the form** opens it: the prompt and each question as its authors print it, each a group of its answers to choose one of (the brand's `sc-choice`), and the authors' attribution line. In one go: nothing is kept until it is sent, and a question left without an answer is asked again with the others kept. **Send to my care team** sends it, once.
- **After sending.** "Answers sent: your care team will go over them with you at your visit." (to a proxy, "<name>'s care team will go over them at the visit."), and the answers given, never a score or band, then or later, with who sent them: "you", "the patient" or "someone the record is shared with", never an account's name, even to another proxy.
- **Not watched as it arrives.** Every form, and the forms page, says: "Not monitored. Your care team reads this form before your visit, not when you send it. If you need help now, call 911. You can call or text 988 at any time to talk with someone at the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline." To a proxy: "Not monitored: <name>'s care team reads this form before the visit, not when you send it. If <name> needs help now, call 911. …". Whenever an alert question is answered so that it needs review (the PHQ-9's question 9 answered other than "Not at all"), the page leads with the help that question names: for the PHQ-9's, "If you are thinking about hurting yourself, or feel you might act on these thoughts, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room now. You can call or text 988 at any time, day or night, to talk with someone at the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Your care team will read your answers before your visit, but not as soon as you send them." It never repeats the answer, which may be read over a shoulder. To a proxy it reads "If <name> is thinking about hurting themselves, or you think they might act on these thoughts, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room now. You can call or text 988 …. <name>'s care team will read these answers before the visit, but not as soon as you send them."
- **Not sent.** Answers sent to a form that can't take them (its appointment cancelled or over while it was being filled in, or moved to a date it isn't open yet) are not stored, and the page says so: "Not sent: your answers were not sent to your care team. This form can no longer be filled in." (or "This form can't be filled in yet."), still with the "Not monitored" notice, and leading with the help when an answer called for it, without the sentence about the care team reading them. **An unsent answer alerts nobody**: nothing was stored, so the database, which delivers the alert with the answers, never saw it; the page's help is all the patient is given. The same holds for a form sent with a question left blank, which is asked again with the help first.
- **A proxy** the patient allowed sensitive items sees the forms and can fill them in, told "You are answering for <name>. The questions ask how they have been."; the answers are recorded as theirs, under their grant, and the clinician sees them as a proxy's. A proxy without sensitive items is given no form, no count and no link: for them the record has none. The Access page says so beside the choice.
- **Off-site.** The forms are read from the cloud copy, and sending is forwarded to the clinic's server; while that can't be reached, a form says "Not sent: forms can't be sent until the clinic's server is back." in place of its questions, and still says where to get help now.

## Clinician workflow

- **Ask.** From the chart's Patient portal section, or an appointment's page, open **Pre-visit forms**. Choose a booked appointment that hasn't ended and a questionnaire, then **Ask for form**. Only a clinician with the patient on their panel asks; each questionnaire is asked once for an appointment.
- **When the patient can answer.** From 7 days before the appointment until its scheduled end, on the database's clock, or, once the patient is checked in and while the visit is in progress, until 12 hours after the scheduled end ("Not answered yet. Open from … until …, while the visit is in progress."), and the portal's "Fill it in by …" says the same: a reschedule moves that window, and a cancellation, a no-show or the visit's end closes it. A booked appointment that isn't checked in closes at its scheduled end. A form already answered stays with its appointment. Nothing is emailed to anyone.
- **Read.** The page says how many forms the patient has and shows none until **Show sensitive items**, which is recorded as a view of sensitive items. Then, for each appointment, each form: the score out of its most ("Score 12 of 27"), its severity band, "Needs follow-up" at 10 or more, "An answer needs review: question 9" for a PHQ-9 whose question 9 was answered other than "Not at all" (any alert question, by its number on the form), who answered and when, and whether it is still open or closed. A trend of each questionnaire's scores, drawn as the vital signs' trends are, shades what lies below 10 and marks a score at or above it as needing follow-up. **Answers** opens a form question by question, in the words it was asked with, with each answer's score and the authors' attribution line, and, above them, "An answer needs review: question 9, "<the question's own words>".".
- **An answer to review.** When a PHQ-9's question 9 is answered other than "Not at all", the database puts an item in the inbox of the appointment's clinician and of each clinician with the patient on their panel (`docs/UC15.md`), before every other item, naming no questionnaire, answer or score and linking to the answers. When none of them can be told (nobody on the panel, and the appointment's clinician's account disabled), every clinician whose account isn't disabled is; when the only clinicians holding it open are later disabled, it is told again the same way; and a clinician who joins the patient's panel while nobody on the team who can still sign in holds an answer's item open is given one. Until each marks their own item done, the chart shows them "Needs review. A pre-visit form has an answer for you to review." The forms aren't watched as they arrive, and the page says so.
- **Read from the inbox.** A clinician holding an open item for an answer to review opens that form's answers from it whether or not the patient is theirs, without claiming emergency access, recorded as a view of sensitive items, until they mark it done; the inbox says so beside **Mark done**: "After you mark this done, the answers open from the patient's chart, which needs the patient on your panel or emergency access." After that they reach the answers as anyone else does: in the inbox's **Done** tab the item keeps its line without the link, and, for a patient on their panel, says "Open from the patient's chart.", as plain words. Without the patient on their panel, the page says "Not one of your patients. <name> is a patient of <the care team> (or: has no care team). To open their chart, you'll need emergency access." Nothing else opens this way: the chart, the forms page and the patient's other forms keep the panel-or-emergency-access gate, and the chart's "Needs review" notice shows only where the chart opens.

## Decisions

- **Questionnaires are data.** `ehr.questionnaires` holds each definition as one JSON document: its questions with LOINC codes, the lists of answers with their scores, the total and its most, the severity bands, the score that needs follow-up, the questions that alert, each naming the help notice the portal shows with such an answer, and the authors' attribution line (`db/reference/36-questionnaires.sql`). The portal words each notice (`FormNotices`); a definition naming one it doesn't know fails `QuestionnaireDefinitionTests`. A clinic adds one there as a reviewed change; there is no screen for it. Questions are single choice; there are no free-text, multiple-choice or skip-logic questions.
- **A request keeps the wording it was asked with.** `ehr.questionnaire_requests` copies the definition when a form is asked for; the answers are scored against that copy, and every page shows it. A later change to the reference data reaches only forms asked for afterwards.
- **The database scores.** `ehr.questionnaire_score` (`db/policies/85-pre-visit-forms.sql`) sums the scored questions' answers and finds the band, the follow-up and the alert. The response's trigger replaces whatever score, band, patient or answerer a client sent, and refuses answers that aren't the form's: a missing question, an unknown one, or an answer that isn't one of its question's.
- **Bands and follow-up, and where they come from.** The PHQ-9's bands are Kroenke et al. 2001's Table 2; the GAD-7's cutpoints of 5, 10 and 15 are the authors' instruction manual's. Below 5 the manual names no GAD-7 band, and the paper that may (Spitzer et al., 2006) couldn't be read, so "Below mild" is this clinic's wording, and the forms page says so. "Needs follow-up" at 10 or more is the manual's yellow flag for the PHQ-9 and its cutpoint for further evaluation for the GAD-7. Scores are not observations: they have no reference range, and nothing flags them High or Low.
- **Everything is a sensitive item, always.** Both tables carry `sensitive`, which can't be false; no row of a form is ever updated or deleted, the tables' owner and a superuser included. What a row trigger can't stop is `TRUNCATE`, which on these tables only their owner holds (as on every table) and which nothing in the system runs. The write audit marks every event of a form, and of an alert's inbox item, as sensitive, so the admin audit view shows none of their values.
- **A late visit's 12 hours.** A checked-in appointment's forms close 12 hours after its scheduled end, the window the vital-signs rule gives a visit (`db/policies/75-results.sql`), rather than at the end of the clinic's day: the clinic's time zone is the app's setting (`Ehr:Clinic:TimeZone`), which the database isn't given, so the database couldn't hold a day's end exactly. The pages show the same time.
- **Who.** Clinicians read every form, as they read charts, once asked; a clinician with the patient on their panel asks. Under emergency access, forms can be read and nothing asked. A clinician given an answer to review reads that one form's answers from their inbox (above); the item must be theirs, which the inbox's row-level security also holds to. Admins see none.
- **Reads.** The forms page is one recorded read of the patient, or one view of sensitive items when they are shown; a form's answers page is always a view of sensitive items.
- **The alert.** Delivered by the database in the same statement as the answers, by a function that runs as the tables' owner and names only the inbox, the panels and the staff accounts. Recipients are the appointment's clinician and each clinician with the patient on their active panel (an assignment that has ended doesn't count) whose account isn't disabled; one locked out for a few minutes is still told. When that finds nobody, every clinician whose account isn't disabled is told, so an answer to review is never stored with nobody told while any clinician account is enabled; with none enabled at all, it is stored with its alert and no item (Limits). The same rule runs again when an admin disables an account holding an open item for an answer (`ehr.follow_disabled_form_alert_holder`, a trigger on the staff accounts that runs as the owner, whatever disables the account): unless a clinician of the team who can still sign in holds it open, it goes to the team's enabled clinicians, or to everyone; the disabled account keeps its item, a lockout of minutes after wrong passwords isn't a disable, and enabling an account again does nothing. An answer being stored, a clinician joining a panel and an account being disabled take one lock between them (shared by answers and joins, alone by a disable; a lockout of minutes takes none), so an answer can't reach only an account being disabled at that moment, nor can a clinician disabled as they join be left its only holder. An answer waits for it at most 5 seconds: behind a disable left open longer (an admin's own SQL session; the Staff page's is one request), the answer is stored all the same, and, without the lock, is told by the rule and also to every clinician whose account isn't disabled, so even then it can't rest with an account being disabled alone. Only a transaction disabling every clinician account at once, held open past those 5 seconds, could leave it with nobody who can sign in, as when no clinician account is enabled (Limits). A clinician who joins the patient's panel while nobody on the team (the panel, or the appointment's clinician) whose account isn't disabled holds an answer's item open is given one, by a trigger on the panels that runs as the owner too and reads the answers besides (`ehr.follow_questionnaire_alert_care_team`). It fires just before step 13d's trigger for refill requests, taking the forms' lock shared and then 13d's care-team lock; the two can't wait in a circle. An answer arriving as a clinician joins reaches the team it sees, or everyone, the one joining among them, so that race can only add an item. A check on the inbox keeps every such item sensitive, whoever writes it. No email is ever sent about a form.
- **The portal's write.** The answers reach the database through a policy and a trigger that runs as the caller, as the portal's own session: the record it is looking at, its own account and grant, and only with sensitive items. The request names the form, never a patient (`PortalSubjectTests` allows `requestId`, read only for the session's patient). A form that doesn't exist, another patient's, one held back from a proxy without sensitive items, one not open yet and one closed are refused alike by the database. The page answers 404 to the first three, after the same statements; one the session can see but that isn't open is answered 409 with the "Not sent" page above, since there is nothing to hide from someone who can see the form, and a blank page would lose the help.
- **The help it gives.** The notices above are fixed, in one place (`FormNotices`), each worded for the patient and for a proxy about the patient, throughout, for a synthetic clinic in the United States: 911, and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, "available 24/7/365", by call, text or chat, "free and confidential", "across the United States and its territories" (988lifeline.org and its "About" page, read 21 November 2026). No clinic number: the synthetic clinic has none, and a clinic line isn't answered at night. The wording and the numbers are the coordinator's, to be read by a clinician before any real use; a clinic outside the United States must change them.
- **Reads.** The portal's forms page and each form's page are a view of sensitive items for the patient and for a proxy allowed them; for a proxy without them, the forms page is a plain read, as any record's would be. The home page's link and count are part of its one read, a plain read, for a proxy allowed sensitive items too. No page with a GET does anything but read and record its read.
- **Off-site.** The pages are read from the cloud copy; asking and sending are forwarded to the clinic's server, and while that can't be reached the clinician's page says "Asking for a pre-visit form needs the clinic's server." and the patient's form says it can't be sent.
- **Desktop.** The offline copy holds no form, and counts them among the sensitive items it doesn't keep.
- **Not built.** An inbox item for a high total without the question-9 answer; one shared "reviewed" record that clears everyone's item; paging or texting anyone; a clinician entering answers for a patient; asking for the same questionnaire twice for one appointment; withdrawing a request; a reminder email; the forms in the FHIR API or in "Download my record" (leaving sensitive answers out of more doors, for Tahseen to decide).

## Limits

- Not validated for clinical use: the questionnaires and their bands are for a demonstration system's synthetic patients.
- A proxy's answers are labeled as a proxy's, but the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 are validated as the patient's own report.
- No page ends a visit yet, so a checked-in appointment's forms close 12 hours after its scheduled end, not when the patient leaves.
- When nobody on the team can be told of an answer to review, every clinician whose account isn't disabled is, which in a large clinic is many inboxes; each marks their own item done.
- With no clinician account enabled at all, an answer to review is stored with its alert and no inbox item: there is nobody who can read it. The forms page shows it to the next clinician who opens it. Not built: telling administrators, who can't read the answers.
