# UC 22 — Answer a pre-visit form

SwiftChart EHR · the expansion plan's step 13e (proposed FR-59) · Tahseen Arnan · a proposed use case, not in the course document

A clinician asks a patient to fill in a questionnaire before a visit; the clinic's database scores the answers, and the clinician reads them, for that visit and over time. The PHQ-9 (depression) and the GAD-7 (anxiety) are built in, worded exactly as their authors publish them (`docs/expansion/reference-data.md`, "Pre-visit questionnaires"). Every form, its request, its answers and its score, is a sensitive item.

## Clinician workflow

- **Ask.** From the chart's Appointments section, or an appointment's page, open **Pre-visit forms**. Choose a booked appointment that hasn't ended and a questionnaire, then **Ask the patient to fill it in**. Only a clinician with the patient on their panel asks; each questionnaire is asked once for an appointment.
- **When the patient can answer.** From 7 days before the appointment until its scheduled end, on the database's clock: a reschedule moves that window, and a cancellation or a no-show closes it. A form already answered stays with its appointment. Nothing is emailed to anyone.
- **Read.** The page says how many forms the patient has and shows none until **Show sensitive items**, which is recorded as a view of sensitive items. Then, for each appointment, each form: the score out of its most ("Score 12 of 27"), its severity band, "Needs follow-up" at 10 or more, "Item 9: needs review" for a PHQ-9 whose question 9 was answered other than "Not at all", who answered and when, and whether it is still open or closed. A trend of each questionnaire's scores, drawn as the vital signs' trends are, shades what lies below 10 and marks a score at or above it as needing follow-up. **Answers** opens a form question by question, in the words it was asked with, with each answer's score and the authors' attribution line.
- **An answer to review.** When a PHQ-9's question 9 is answered other than "Not at all", the database puts an item in the inbox of the appointment's clinician and of each clinician with the patient on their panel (`docs/UC15.md`), before every other item, naming no questionnaire, answer or score and linking to the answers. Until each marks their own item done, the chart shows them "Needs review: a pre-visit form has an answer for you to review." The forms aren't watched as they arrive, and the page says so.

## Decisions

- **Questionnaires are data.** `ehr.questionnaires` holds each definition as one JSON document: its questions with LOINC codes, the lists of answers with their scores, the total and its most, the severity bands, the score that needs follow-up, the questions that alert, and the authors' attribution line (`db/reference/36-questionnaires.sql`). A clinic adds one there as a reviewed change; there is no screen for it. Questions are single choice; there are no free-text, multiple-choice or skip-logic questions.
- **A request keeps the wording it was asked with.** `ehr.questionnaire_requests` copies the definition when a form is asked for; the answers are scored against that copy, and every page shows it. A later change to the reference data reaches only forms asked for afterwards.
- **The database scores.** `ehr.questionnaire_score` (`db/policies/85-pre-visit-forms.sql`) sums the scored questions' answers and finds the band, the follow-up and the alert. The response's trigger replaces whatever score, band, patient or answerer a client sent, and refuses answers that aren't the form's: a missing question, an unknown one, or an answer that isn't one of its question's.
- **Bands and follow-up, and where they come from.** The PHQ-9's bands are Kroenke et al. 2001's Table 2; the GAD-7's cutpoints of 5, 10 and 15 are the authors' instruction manual's. Below 5 the manual names no GAD-7 band, and the paper that may (Spitzer et al., 2006) couldn't be read, so "Below mild" is this clinic's wording, and the forms page says so. "Needs follow-up" at 10 or more is the manual's yellow flag for the PHQ-9 and its cutpoint for further evaluation for the GAD-7. Scores are not observations: they have no reference range, and nothing flags them High or Low.
- **Everything is a sensitive item, always.** Both tables carry `sensitive`, which can't be false; nothing changes a form, the tables' owner included. The write audit marks every event of a form, and of an alert's inbox item, as sensitive, so the admin audit view shows none of their values.
- **Who.** Clinicians read every form, as they read charts, once asked; a clinician with the patient on their panel asks. Under emergency access, forms can be read and nothing asked. Admins see none.
- **Reads.** The forms page is one recorded read of the patient, or one view of sensitive items when they are shown; a form's answers page is always a view of sensitive items.
- **The alert.** Delivered by the database in the same statement as the answers, by a function that runs as the tables' owner and names only the inbox, the panels and the staff accounts. Recipients are the appointment's clinician and each clinician with the patient on their active panel whose account isn't disabled; one locked out for a few minutes is still told. No email is ever sent about a form.
- **Off-site.** The pages are read from the cloud copy; asking is forwarded to the clinic's server, and while that can't be reached the page says "Asking for a pre-visit form needs the clinic's server."
- **Desktop.** The offline copy holds no form, and counts them among the sensitive items it doesn't keep.
- **Not built.** An inbox item for a high total without the question-9 answer; one shared "reviewed" record that clears everyone's item; paging or texting anyone; a clinician entering answers for a patient; asking for the same questionnaire twice for one appointment; withdrawing a request; a reminder email; the forms in the FHIR API or in "Download my record" (leaving sensitive answers out of more doors, for Tahseen to decide).

## Limits

- Not validated for clinical use: the questionnaires and their bands are for a demonstration system's synthetic patients.
- A proxy's answers are labeled as a proxy's, but the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 are validated as the patient's own report.
- A recipient whose account is disabled after the appointment was booked, with nobody else on the patient's panel, means nobody is told of an answer to review; the form still shows it.
