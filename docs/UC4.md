# UC 4 — View a patient's chart: editing its lists

SwiftChart EHR · UC 4 is Diego Valenzuela's · this part (the expansion plan's step 2) by Tahseen Arnan

This page covers what the expansion added to the chart: editing allergies, medications and diagnoses, their standard codes, their history, and sensitive items. The chart itself is described in the README.

## Clinician workflow

Open a patient on your panel. Each of **Allergies**, **Medications** and **Diagnoses** has an **Add** button and, after each entry, a **Change** link, while the clinic's server can be reached.

- **Add** opens a form with the patient's banner. Type at least two letters of the name and select from the list: diagnoses are ICD-10-CM (a code can be typed too, as "I10" or "e119", and saving it saves that diagnosis), medications are RxNorm clinical drugs, and allergens are RxNorm ingredients or FDA substances. The form refuses a name that isn't one of them. Under the field, the search says "No matches." when nothing matches, and "Signed out. …" if your session has ended.
- **Change** opens the same form filled in. Nothing is deleted: give the entry another status: an allergy inactive or resolved, a medication on hold, stopped, completed or entered in error, a diagnosis in remission, inactive or resolved. The chart lists what the patient has now.
- An entry recorded before there were codes keeps its name until you change it.
- **After saving,** the chart says so: "Diagnosis saved.", or, for an entry given a status that takes it off the chart, "Medication saved. … is now stopped." A sensitive entry the chart is leaving out isn't named: "Diagnosis saved. Hidden as sensitive."
- **Posting a form twice saves one entry.** An "Add" form carries its new entry's ID from when it opened, so a double click, or going back and saving again, changes that entry rather than adding a second. (Two people changing the same entry at once still isn't warned about: the later save wins.)
- **The keyboard follows what happened:** after a save the chart's notice has the focus, and a form that comes back with mistakes has the cursor in the first field to fix.
- **When a save can't happen,** nothing is lost. If the database refuses it, the form comes back with what you entered and "Not saved. The entry changed, or this isn't one of your patients.". If the patient left your panel after you opened the form, a page says "Not saved. This isn't one of your patients." and shows what you typed, to copy, without anything about the patient.
- **Allergies are in one of three states.** "No allergy information recorded" means nobody has asked. **Record no known allergies** records that someone asked and there are none, shown as "No known allergies (recorded <date> by <name>)", and the chart confirms it ("Saved. No known allergies."). If a colleague recorded an allergy since you opened the chart, it isn't recorded, and the chart says why. Adding an allergy clears "No known allergies" by itself. The chart, the patient banner and the desktop app's offline copy each show the state.
- **History of allergies, medications and diagnoses**, under the lists, shows every entry added and every change, newest first, with who and when, in the form's words ("Status: Active → On hold", "Criticality: not recorded → High risk").
- **An allergy a dictation proposed** (the expansion plan's step 15; `docs/UC13.md`). When a clinician dictates a plain instruction to add an allergy ("add penicillin to her allergy list"), the dictation's review lists it as a proposal, and **Open in the allergy form** opens **Add allergy** as `/patients/{id}/allergies?proposal={proposal}`, under "Proposed from dictation. Check every field. Nothing here was filled in from it." and the clinician's words after "As heard:", marked in the sentence they said them in, with what was heard after them after "Heard after it:" (step 15.1). Nothing in the form comes from the words: the substance is picked from the codes as for any allergy, and the allergy added is active. **Save allergy** adds it and decides the proposal with it, in one transaction, then goes back to the review; the database links the proposal only to an active coded allergy the clinician deciding it added for that patient since the transaction deciding it began, as its first history row says (`db/policies/55-ai-review.sql`). That is the allergy this transaction added, the only one the app links, or one the same clinician added for the same patient in another of their transactions committed meanwhile: their own entry for that patient either way. If the proposal was decided first, nothing is saved, the allergy included. Dictation never proposes removing, resolving or changing an allergy: those stay listed as not carried over, for the clinician to do here. The `proposal` in the address doesn't survive a sign-in. Test cases TC-UC4-33 and TC-UC4-34.

**Vital signs** (the expansion plan's step 9, by Tahseen Arnan). The chart's **Vital signs** section lists the newest value of each sign, with High or Low where it is outside its reference range, and links to the patient's vital signs page, `/patients/{id}/vitals`. A blood pressure is shown as one value, "126/80 mmHg", from one reading, tagged High or Low when either number is outside its range (step 9c):

- **Flowsheet:** the latest six sets side by side, newest first, each sign with its unit; a value outside its reference range is tagged High or Low. Blood pressure is one row.
- **Trends:** a small chart for each sign with two or more values; blood pressure keeps a chart for each of its two numbers, drawn from whole readings. The shaded band is the reference range, a value outside it is a diamond, and the sentence under the chart says what it shows. **The values as a table** opens every number behind it. The charts are drawn by the server as SVG: no script.
- **Record vital signs**, first on the page: blood pressure, heart rate, temperature, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, weight and height. Enter what was taken and leave the rest empty. Body mass index is worked out from the weight and height. Only a clinician with the patient on their panel records; off-site the set is forwarded to the clinic's server, and recording is not offered while that server can't be reached.
- A set taken during a visit, or up to fifteen minutes before it began, belongs to that visit: the database ties it to the patient's latest visit that began no later than fifteen minutes after it was taken, within the twelve hours before, and the chart's latest visit then shows its blood pressure and heart rate. A set sent twice is recorded once.
- **The visit is decided from the time the set was taken alone** (step 9c, and Tahseen's answer of 20 November), not the moment it is typed. Vital signs taken in the waiting room up to fifteen minutes before check-in belong to the visit whether they are typed before or after the check-in: checking the patient in ties the sets already recorded to the visit it starts, both those with no visit and those tied to a visit of the patient's that began earlier. Taken earlier than that, they aren't tied to it, and the chart's visit line shows them as not taken at the visit. A visit starts only by checking the patient in, at that moment: the database gives it its own clock's time, whatever is sent, so no visit can take a set it wasn't checked in for. Every value for the same patient and time, a correction included, takes the same visit, so a check-in never splits a reading.
- Each sign has bounds no person is outside of (a temperature from 20 to 45 °C, for example); the form and the database both refuse a value beyond them.
- Times are typed and shown on the clinic's clock, labeled with its zone (the plan's step 10d), and stored in UTC. A set of readings is still named by its moment in UTC in the link that marks it entered in error. A time the clocks skip on the day they go forward is refused; one that happens twice when they go back is taken as the first of the two, so a reading taken in the second of those hours can't be typed as such.
- **Known limit:** the age that picks a reading's reference range is worked out by the database on the UTC day the reading was taken, not the clinic's day. A reading taken in the evening before a birthday that moves the patient to the next age band (the 1st, 6th, 12th or 18th) can be flagged against the next band's range.
- **A wrong reading** (step 9b) is marked **entered in error**, never deleted or edited. Under each set on the flowsheet, **Mark as entered in error** opens a page that lists the set's readings: tick the wrong ones, give the reason, and choose **Mark as entered in error**. The readings stay in the record with your name, the time and the reason, and are left out of the flowsheet, the trends, the chart, the offline copy and the portal. **Show entries in error** lists them on the flowsheet struck through, with the reason. It can't be undone. The body mass index isn't a reading to tick: marking a weight or height also marks the body mass index worked out from it. The flowsheet lists the six latest sets; **Show earlier sets** lists up to 24, as many as a trend draws, so any of them can be corrected.
- **A blood pressure is one reading** (step 9c): its systolic and diastolic, entered together, share a reading, stand together, and are marked entered in error together. **Mark as entered in error** lists it as one item, and marking it marks both numbers, with the same reason, each recorded as its own change. **Show entries in error** strikes a blood pressure through as one value; a number marked in error alone before readings existed shows a dash for the other half, "150/— entered in error: …".
- **The correct value** is a new reading: type the set again for the same time. A value that already stands for that moment is left as it is, so only what was wrong is recorded again; a weight or height corrected by itself gets a new body mass index. A blood pressure is typed again whole, both numbers, as a new reading.
- **One value of a sign stands at a time** (step 9c): a different value for a sign that already has one for that time is refused, before or after the patient is checked in, with "A value is already recorded for that time. Mark it as entered in error first." (for a blood pressure, "A blood pressure is already recorded for that time."). The database refuses it too.
- **Ranges by age.** Heart rate, respiratory rate and blood pressure are flagged against the range for the patient's age on the day they were taken, and a child's blood pressure for their sex too (step 9d): a child's heart and respiratory rates against Fleming et al.'s centiles for 13 age groups, a child's blood pressure from 1 year against the AAP's screening table for the year of age, and from the 18th birthday the adults' ranges (`docs/expansion/reference-data.md` has the numbers and their sources). A child whose sex is other or unknown is held to the lower of the boys' and girls' values. Temperature and oxygen saturation have one range. A trend marks each point against the range it was recorded with, and says so when a child has since moved into another age group.
- **A child's blood pressure needs follow-up, not High** (step 9d). The AAP's table is for screening only, so a reading at or above its value is tagged **Needs follow-up** on the flowsheet, the chart and the trends, never High, and nothing below it is flagged. Its trend's shaded band is below the screening value, and the sentence under the chart says "Needs follow-up at or above 98 mmHg." Under 1 year a blood pressure has no range.
- **On the chart**, each sign's newest value has a small mark, and the words for a screen reader, when it is up or down from the reading before it. A blood pressure has one only when its two numbers didn't move opposite ways ("down from 148/92").
- A vital sign is an observation with its LOINC code and UCUM unit, like a lab result. It can't be edited or deleted. The patient sees their vital signs in the portal at once (UC 6).
- A visit's blood pressure and heart rate, which used to be the visit's own columns, are now observations linked to the visit. The chart's latest visit and the desktop app's offline copy show them as before, the blood pressure's two numbers from one reading (step 9c). The offline copy does not carry the full flowsheet.

### Sensitive items

Mental-health, substance-use and some other diagnoses are sensitive by their code (`docs/expansion/reference-data.md` has the ranges), and you can mark any diagnosis or medication sensitive. On the form, a diagnosis sensitive by its code has its **Sensitive** box checked and fixed, with the hint "Always sensitive for this code". (A new diagnosis is checked once it's saved or the form comes back, since the code isn't known while you type; the database marks it either way.) The chart leaves sensitive items out and says how many it left out. **Show sensitive items** reloads the chart with them, each tagged "Sensitive", and that view is recorded in the audit log as `read_sensitive`. The history page follows the same rule, and so does opening a sensitive entry's form. While sensitive items are shown, going to the history or a form and back keeps them shown. Sensitive items are never in the patient banner, and the desktop app doesn't keep them offline.

Pre-visit forms (the plan's step 13e, `docs/UC22.md`) are sensitive items always. The chart's Patient portal section links to the patient's **Pre-visit forms** page, with how many there are, and never lists them; that page shows them only when asked, recorded the same way. A clinician with a pre-visit form's answer to review in their own inbox sees a danger notice at the top of the chart, "Pre-visit form to review.", until they mark the item done. The desktop app keeps no form and counts them among the sensitive items it doesn't keep.

## Decisions

- **Who.** Clinicians only, and only for a patient on their panel, as for orders and notes. Under emergency access the chart and the history can be read, and nothing can be changed.
- **Data.** The existing tables, `ehr.allergy_intolerances`, `ehr.medication_statements` and `ehr.conditions`, gain `sensitive` (the last two), and `severity` and `code_system` (allergies). `ehr_app` gets INSERT and UPDATE on them, and never DELETE. Row-level security: a clinician, for a patient on their active panel. A trigger refuses moving an entry to another patient or ID.
- **Codes.** `ehr.clinical_codes`, reference data that nothing at runtime changes, loaded from `db/reference`. A new entry stores the code, its system (for allergies) and the source's own name.
- **History.** `ehr.chart_history`, written only by a trigger that runs as its owner, so it can't be skipped or edited. Clinicians read it. The write-audit trigger records the same changes for admins.
- **Sensitive.** The database sets the flag for a diagnosis whose code is in a sensitive range, whatever the form sent, so it can't be cleared. The reader leaves sensitive rows out unless asked, and records the asking as its own audit action, on-site and off-site.
- **"No known allergies".** An entry of its own, with the app's own marker; the SNOMED CT code it used before is gone. The database keeps the list to one state: it refuses "No known allergies" for a patient with an active allergy, and makes it inactive when an active allergy is added, in the same transaction, by the same clinician, with the history and the write audit recording both. A patient has at most one active "No known allergies": the guard trigger refuses a second, and a unique partial index (`ix_allergy_intolerances_one_no_known_allergy`) holds it even with the triggers off. The chart treats a second click as already recorded.
- **Admins and sensitive values.** The audit page shows that a sensitive item changed and who changed it, never its values, and the same for the events written before the item was marked. The runtime roles can't read the audit table's values column at all; the page asks `ehr.shown_audit_details`, which answers only admins and leaves sensitive items out. The full record stays in the table. See `docs/UC12.md`.
- **Banner.** The patient banner now shows allergies on the note, dictation and review pages, the history and the discontinued orders too, read with the patient in the one read each page already records; on the history and the discontinued orders it also carries the emergency-access tag while that access lasts, as on the chart.
- **Search.** `/codes` returns up to 20 names that start with what was typed, then names with a word that starts with it, then diagnoses by code. It reads nothing about a patient. With today's subset it is a small scan; the full lists in step 2a need an index for it.
- **Off-site.** Reads come from the cloud copy; a save is forwarded to the clinic's server. While that can't be reached the chart offers no Add or Change, under the layout's read-only band.
- **Desktop.** The offline chart already held the three lists. It doesn't hold sensitive items, and says how many aren't kept.

## Limits

- A note is marked sensitive when it is signed, and an addendum when it is written (`docs/UC14.md`); a signed note can't be marked afterwards.
- "No known allergies" is cleared by adding an allergy; there is no button to take it back on its own.
- Allergies and diagnoses can't be marked entered in error yet; a mistaken one is made inactive. (Medications can.)
- A medication isn't sensitive by rule; that waits for drug-class data.
- A medication's refill requests (step 13d, `docs/UC21.md`) follow it: marking it sensitive, or unmarking it, marks its requests the same. Stopping it, marking it entered in error, or changing it to another drug or strength, leaves a waiting request in the care team's inboxes, to be declined with a message: it can no longer be approved.
- The reference codes are a subset until step 2a.
- The suggestions are the browser's own list, which differs a little between browsers.
