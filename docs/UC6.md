# UC 6 — View test results

SwiftChart EHR · Simar Rekhi · CS 3354 Software Engineering, Fall 2026

## Patient workflow

Sign in to the portal and choose **Results** in the navigation, or **See your test results** on the portal home (`/portal/results`). Each released result shows the test, the date the sample was taken (in UTC, labelled, as every date in the app is), the value with its unit, and whether it is low, normal or high. Open a result to read its normal range, what the result means in plain language, what the test measures, and any note from the care team. With no released results, the page says so and points the patient to their care team.

## Decisions

- **Who.** Patients only, through the Portal area's Patient policy. The page takes the patient from the session, never from the request: it has no ID parameter, which `PortalSubjectTests` checks for every portal handler.
- **Data.** One table, `ehr.observations`, shaped on FHIR's Observation (laboratory category): patient, LOINC code, display name, status, value, unit, reference range, sample time, release time (`issued_at`), the care team's note, and the lab order it answers (`order_id`, optional). The order link is keyed on `(order_id, patient_id)`, so a result can't name another patient's order. Check constraints hold the status to FHIR's codes and the range's low end at or below its high end.
- **Released.** A patient sees a result only once it has a release time and its status is `final`, `amended` or `corrected`. The rule is in the row-level security policy (`observations_own_released_select`) and repeated in `PhiReader.ResultsAsync`.
- **Database access.** Row-level security is enabled and forced. Clinicians read every result, as for the rest of the chart; a patient reads only their own released ones. Grants: `ehr_app` and `ehr_read` SELECT only. Nothing at runtime writes results yet. The standard write-audit trigger records any write.
- **Not cached.** The results page and the portal home send `Cache-Control: no-store`, as the note pages do, so Back after signing out on a shared computer doesn't show them again.
- **Reads.** Through `PhiReader.ResultsAsync`. Opening the page is one recorded read of the patient, as for the chart. `CloudAuditEvents` did not need widening.
- **Off-site.** Like every page that reads PHI, the results page is served from the cloud replica when the on-site server is down. It shows the read-only banner, and its read goes to the cloud audit queue and arrives on-site with source `cloud`.
- **Plain language (FR-16).** `Results/ResultExplanation.cs` flags the value against its range, writes a range with one open end the way people say it ("below 100 mg/dL"), drops the zeros the database keeps, and says what 14 common tests measure, by LOINC code.
- **Seed.** The synthetic clinic gives each patient up to six results from 13 common tests after their visits. 90% are released, and some results outside their range carry a care-team note. Seeded results don't link to orders, since the seed has none.

## Limits

- Nothing writes results yet: they will come from a lab interface, which isn't built. The seed and the tests insert them directly.
- The portal doesn't show which order a result answers, and the clinician's chart doesn't list results yet.
- Results are numeric only. A text result such as "negative" gets no flag and the "ask your care team" wording.
- Dates are shown in UTC, labelled, since the app has no clinic time zone yet. A sample taken on a Chicago evening shows the next day's date.

**Visit summaries are built** (the expansion plan's step 13a, by Tahseen Arnan). **See your visit summaries** on the portal's home opens **Visit summaries** (`/portal/summaries`): what the care team wrote for the patient after a visit, from a signed note, newest first. Each is headed with its date, says who wrote it, and has three parts, "What we talked about", "What we found" and "What happens next", as written, line breaks kept. The notes themselves, and their addenda, never reach the portal: a summary is written for the patient (`docs/UC14.md`).
- **The date** is the day the visit's notes were signed, printed as this page's results are dated ("Nov 2, 2026 UTC"), and the page says so. A note has no link to its visit, so the visit's own date isn't known; the day it was signed is the nearest date the record holds, and it never changes.
- **Sensitive summaries** (written from a sensitive note, or from one with a sensitive addendum, or marked by the clinician) are left out until the patient asks: "Sensitive items hidden: 1 visit summary is not shown. Show sensitive summaries". The link is a plain link, so asking is a read that the cloud app answers too, and it is recorded as a view of sensitive items. Shown, the page says "Sensitive items shown: your view of them is recorded.", and each one is tagged "Sensitive". It is the portal's first page to show sensitive items.
- **A proxy** sees the summaries of the record they are acting for. Sensitive ones only when the patient allowed them sensitive items, and then only after asking, as the patient does. Without that, sensitive summaries don't exist for them: no count, no notice, the same page as if there were none; and a summary that becomes sensitive is gone from their next page.
- **A withdrawn summary** leaves a placeholder where it was, by its date: "A visit summary from Nov 2, 2026 UTC was withdrawn by your care team. Contact the clinic if you have questions." Nothing else of it reaches the portal: not its text, its reason or who withdrew it. A new summary of the same visit stands beside the placeholder, before it. A withdrawn sensitive summary's placeholder is shown only to someone who could have seen the summary, once they ask. If the patient or a proxy had viewed the summary before it was withdrawn, the patient is emailed as for a new one (Tahseen's decisions of 21 November 2026).
- **Telling the patient.** When a summary is released, the patient's own portal account gets an email, "There is something new in your patient portal", with a button to the page and nothing about the summary; at most one a day. Never a proxy, as for messages.
- **Reads.** Opening the page is one recorded read of the patient (a view of sensitive items when they were asked for) and a read of each summary shown: that is how the clinic knows a summary had been viewed. At the page's limit of 50 summaries that is 51 reads a view, written in one statement on-site and given to the cloud app's queue in one write off-site. The portal reads no summary's row: the database gives it each standing summary's date, who wrote it, its three parts and whether it is sensitive, and a withdrawn one's date (`ehr.standing_visit_summaries`, `ehr.withdrawn_visit_summaries`), and nothing else, not why or by whom one was marked sensitive. Off-site it reads the cloud copy, and a view reaches the clinic's audit log when the cloud app ships it, within seconds while the link to the clinic is up; while the link is down the view waits in the cloud app until it is back, so a summary withdrawn in between counts as not viewed and the patient isn't emailed.
- **Not cached,** and no ID, code or record number anywhere in its markup.

**Download my record is built** (the expansion plan's step 13b, by Tahseen Arnan). **Download my record** on the portal's home opens a page (`/portal/record`) that offers a copy of the record the portal is looking at: the patient's own, or, for a proxy, the record they act for.
- **The largest change in what a patient sees.** Until this step the portal showed no allergies, medications or diagnoses. The copy holds them, as the database lets the portal read them (the `*_own_select` policies, `db/policies/30-rls.sql`): every allergy, every medication but one entered in error, every diagnosis, with sensitive ones under the rules below. For a proxy, what their grant allows, under either scope: the scope decides messages, not the record.
- **As a file (FHIR).** One FHIR R4 `collection` Bundle, saved as `health-record-<date>.json` (the date in UTC; no name, number or ID), of the patient, their allergies, medications, diagnoses, test results, vital signs (a blood pressure as one reading) and appointments, from the same mapping as the clinicians' FHIR API (UC 19, `src/Ehr.Web/Fhir`). `application/fhir+json`, never cached, never readable by another site. It doesn't hold visit summaries yet, and the page says so.
- **As a printable page** (`/portal/record/print`): the same, with the visit summaries as **Visit summaries** shows them (withdrawn ones as their placeholders), laid out to print or to save as a PDF. Printed, the paper has no app bar and no buttons (`docs/brand`: printed pages); a proxy's printout keeps the band that says whose record it is, and an off-site one the band that says it is read-only, as every printed page does.
- **What it never holds.** Results and vital signs come only once released, as **Results** shows them (released, final, amended or corrected), so never a value entered in error or a result not yet released; nor a medication entered in error. The database holds both rules for every portal session, the patient's and a proxy's (`observations_own_released_select`, and `medication_statements_own_select` since the step's review), and the reader asks them too. Allergies, diagnoses and appointments have no such status. An appointment's cancellation reason, which the portal doesn't show, is left out. Never notes, addenda, drafts, orders, messages, or anything from the audit log.
- **No identifier of the clinic's.** Every id in the file is made for that file alone (`urn:uuid:`), and differs in every download; the clinician of an appointment is named, not referred to. The patient's own record number is kept. A system that imports two downloads can match the patient by the record number, but not an entry by its id.
- **Long records.** Each kind of entry is cut at its newest 600, as a search of the FHIR API is, results and vital signs apart; the file says what it left out in an OperationOutcome, and the page in words. The printable page has the newest 50 visit summaries at most, as **Visit summaries** has: **Download my record** says so, and when it shows 50 the printable page says "This page shows at most the 50 newest visit summaries." (the reader doesn't say whether more were left out).
- **Sensitive items** are left out unless "Include sensitive items" is ticked (the patient, or a proxy the patient allowed them): the file then marks them restricted, and the page tags them. The file never says whether any were left out; the printable page says how many, on screen and not on paper. A proxy without sensitive items is offered no box, and gets the same copy as if there were none, asking or not.
- **Said before anything is downloaded:** the file and the page hold health information, and where they go once downloaded or printed is the person's responsibility.
- **A link can't choose for the person.** The sign-in cookies are never sent with a request from another site, so a link from anywhere arrives signed out and is sent to sign in. Both sign-ins, the portal's and the staff's, go on to the page the link named without its query, whether the person signs in there or was signed in already, and the staff sign-in after its code or a first enrolment too. The one thing kept is a patient search's criteria (name, date of birth and MRN) through the staff sign-in, so a search left when a session ended comes back; never a handler or a request for sensitive items. So `/portal/record?handler=File&sensitive=true` opens **Download my record** with its warning and the box unticked, even through the staff sign-in in a browser that also holds a portal session, and `?sensitive=true` on the printable page or **Visit summaries** opens them without sensitive items. Whatever the person wants, they ask for on the page. No answer has a CORS header, so no other site can read one.
- **Reads.** A download of the file is recorded as `download`, or `download_sensitive` with sensitive items, apart from a view; opening the printable page records what **Visit summaries** records (a read of the patient, of sensitive items when asked for, and a read of each summary shown, so a summary seen on paper counts as viewed). The page that offers them reads nothing. A HEAD of the file or the printable page, which would read and record with nothing sent, is refused (405, `Allow: GET`) before anything is read. Off-site both come from the cloud copy, recorded in its audit queue before they are sent.

**Refill requests are built** (the expansion plan's step 13d, by Tahseen Arnan; proposed FR-58). **Renew a medication** on the portal's home opens `/portal/refills`: the patient asks the care team to renew one of the medications on their record, and sees what became of each request. The care team's side is `docs/UC21.md`.
- **What can be asked for.** The record's active medications coded as a product the clinic orders (its order catalog has an item under the medication's drug code), each with how it is taken, but one with a request waiting already: one open request a medication. A medication that was stopped or entered in error isn't offered, nor one with no code or coded by its ingredient alone, which has no product to renew (the page says to call the clinic for those). The patient may add a note of up to 500 characters; nothing else is asked, and nothing is sent to a pharmacy.
- **Who.** The patient, and someone they share their record with only when their access includes messages: asking is writing to the care team. Under the record alone the portal's home has no link, the page sends them to the home, and the database refuses the request. Sensitive medications, and requests for them, are left out until "Show sensitive items" is asked for (recorded as a view of sensitive items), and a proxy the patient didn't allow sensitive items never sees or asks for one; a request follows its medication, so when the clinic marks a medication sensitive, its requests are gone from such a proxy's page at once.
- **Nobody to tell.** A request goes to the inbox of each clinician with the patient on their panel (one an admin disabled doesn't count; one locked out for a while after failed sign-ins does), and follows the panel as it changes: a request left with nobody on it waits, shown waiting here, for whoever joins next. With none when asking, the page says so ("No care team yet:") and offers no form, and the database refuses a request sent anyway: nobody would see it.
- **What the patient sees.** Each request, latest first: the medication as it was named when asked, when, "by you" or, for the patient, "by someone you share your record with", the note, and its state: Waiting; Approved, with the date and what the care team ordered, the same medication; or Declined, with the date and the care team's message. The page never names the clinician who answered, and shows no ID of anything: a medication is chosen by a reference the app protects for the record being looked at, which the database checks again.
- **Telling the patient.** When the care team answers a request, the patient's own portal account gets the email visit summaries send, "There is something new in your patient portal", naming nothing, with a link to the portal's home; at most one a day for answers to refill requests. Nothing is sent when a request is made, and never to a proxy.
- **The patient's side never takes a patient from the request,** and the post needs the page's form token.
- **Reads.** Opening the page is one recorded read of the patient (a view of sensitive items when they were asked for); a HEAD of it is refused (405, `Allow: GET`) before anything is read. Off-site it is read from the cloud copy and a request is forwarded to the clinic's server; while that can't be reached the form is replaced by "Read-only: requests can't be sent until the clinic's server is back.", and a request sent from a page opened before gets the "Not saved" page with the note to copy. Not cached.
