# UC 12 — Open a chart through emergency access

SwiftChart EHR · Luke Garcia · CS 3354 Software Engineering, Fall 2026

## Clinician workflow

Search for the patient and open their chart. A patient on the clinician's panel opens straight away. For anyone else, the chart sends the clinician to **Emergency access** (`/patients/{id}/reason`), which shows nothing about the patient and asks why the chart is needed, in 10 to 500 characters. **Open the chart** records the reason and opens the chart with a notice that the access is recorded for review and when it ends. For four hours the chart, its notes and its note pages open to that clinician without asking again; after that the reason is asked for again. Admins see each use on **Audit** (`/admin/audit`), marked **Review**, and **Emergency access only** lists just those. The patient, and anyone they share sensitive items with, can see in the portal's **Who viewed my record** that the clinician used emergency access and when, but never the reason typed; the reason page says so (the expansion plan's step 13c, `docs/UC6.md`).

## Decisions

- **Who.** Clinicians only: the Patients folder of the Clinical area has the Clinician policy, so an admin is sent to `/denied` and someone signed out to `/sign-in`, whatever address they type.
- **Gate.** In the app, not the database (`Ehr.Domain/Patients/BreakGlass.cs`). Row-level security lets every clinician read every patient, as the team decided; the reason and the audit log are the control. The chart, the note pages and the reason page all check the panel first, then a grant that hasn't ended.
- **Reason.** Trimmed as it is bound (`Reason.cshtml.cs`), then checked: required, at least 10 characters, at most 500, each with its own message. `BreakGlass.GrantAsync` checks the same lengths again, so no other caller can record a grant without a reason.
- **Recorded twice, in one transaction.** A `break_glass` audit event with the reason, which is what admins review, and a row in `ehr.break_glass_accesses` that lets the clinician back in for four hours. The table's write-audit trigger records the row as well. Every chart view is a `read` event through the PHI reader, and a change from the chart, such as **Add to my patients**, is the trigger's `insert`.
- **Off-site.** The cloud app forwards the reason to the on-prem app, which saves it, over HTTPS only: the cloud app refuses an `Ehr:OnPrem:Url` that isn't `https://` (`Forwarding/OnPremApp.cs`). The chart's reads are served from the replica and reach the on-prem log with source `cloud`. With the clinic's server unreachable, nothing can be recorded, so emergency access isn't offered and the page says why; a reason typed just as the link drops is shown again on the "Not saved" page to copy.
- **Not cached.** The chart sends `Cache-Control: no-store`, as the note pages do, so it isn't kept on a shared computer's disk and Back after signing out doesn't show it.

## Limits

- An admin can see and filter emergency accesses but can't mark one as reviewed: the **Review** tag stays on every one. Recording a review would need a new table or column, with its migration and policies.
- The reason is stored as plain text in `ehr.break_glass_accesses` and `ehr.audit_events`; it is encrypted at rest only by the disk (TC-UC12-05).
- The four hours run from the grant. There is no way to end a grant early, and a new reason given while one is active adds a second grant.

## Sensitive items in the audit log (the expansion plan's step 2b, by Tahseen Arnan)

Admins have no access to patient data; the values a write changed, in an event's details, were the one place they saw any. For a sensitive item (a diagnosis or medication marked sensitive, `docs/UC4.md`) they no longer do.

- **What the page shows.** A write to a sensitive item is listed like any other, with when, who, what and which row, a "Sensitive" tag, and "A sensitive item changed. Its values aren't shown here." The copies the lists' history keeps are listed too, without values.
- **What the database does.** The write-audit trigger marks the event (`audit_events.sensitive`), and, when an item becomes sensitive, marks that item's earlier events. The runtime roles can't read `audit_events.details`. The page gets values from `ehr.shown_audit_details`, which answers only an admin and never for a marked event. The full record stays in the table, for a restore or a legal request handled outside the app.
- **Nothing unmarks an event,** and an event takes no other change, from anyone: a trigger refuses it.

Test case TC-UC12-07 (`tests/Ehr.Web.Tests/TestCases/UC12/TC_UC12_07_SensitiveValuesHidden.cs`) checks the page; `tests/Ehr.Security.Tests/SensitiveAuditSecurityTests.cs` checks the database.
