## Development base and PR dependency

PR #39 implements UC 14. It is `main` plus the single UC 14 commit, on the branch `feature/uc14-clinical-notes-v2`, and it replaces PR #29, which was built on the UC 8 branch before that branch was rebased.

Both dependencies are merged: **UC 7 (PR #28: cached charts, offline drafts and recordings)** and **UC 8 (PR #38: sync offline drafts and recordings)**, which replaced PR #30 and the earlier combined PR #26. UC 8 supplies the ClinicalDraft entity, migration, Body/version/receipt contract, draft sync API, RLS and audit triggers used by this implementation. No competing draft model or replacement migration is needed. UC 8's one additional security expectation (no draft reads by ehr_read in UC 8 alone) is explicitly extended by UC 14's SELECT grant for audited cloud draft viewing; author/panel RLS and read-only-role write restrictions remain enforced and tested.

Nothing remains to merge first. PR #39's base is `main`, so its diff contains only UC 14.

## Clinician workflow

Open a patient on your panel and choose **New note**. Subjective receives focus. Enter Subjective, Objective, Assessment and Plan (the expansion plan's step 14, below); edits are autosaved to the primary after a 750 ms pause. Save status distinguishes Unsaved, Saving, Saved and Save Failed. Saves are serialized and use optimistic versions. Ctrl/Cmd+S saves explicitly. Leaving with unconfirmed changes prompts the browser's unsaved-work warning.

Your chart lists private draft links and finalized note links. Reopening a draft loads its persisted fields. **Sign note…** saves and validates the current text, then opens a read-only confirmation of that saved version. Check the confirmation box and choose **Confirm and sign note**. The final record displays the signing clinician, the time of signing (on the clinic's clock, labeled) and its read-only sections. A later edit invalidates an older confirmation.

An emergency chart remains readable under the existing reason/access gate. Add the patient to your panel to write notes; this preserves UC 8's active-panel restriction. Only a draft's author can reopen, edit or sign it. Signed notes follow the existing clinician chart-access model; they are not exposed to the patient portal in this use case.

Requests use Razor antiforgery tokens and the existing clinical area policy. There is no new frontend framework and no note workflow writes to browser localStorage/sessionStorage. The chart, chart-access reason, and note pages omit htmx, which otherwise writes patient URLs to sessionStorage during initialization even when history caching is disabled. Other pages retain their existing htmx behavior. Requests failing during save/sign keep the current page and its text. Ambiguous transport/commit failures say that confirmation failed, rather than claiming that signing succeeded or that a possibly committed operation did not occur. Retrying an acknowledged saved version is idempotent. Refreshing or closing a page can still lose work the server has never confirmed; this is why the warning and explicit status exist. With JavaScript disabled, explicit save/review/sign still work; autosave and keeping the form visible during transport failures require JavaScript.

## Addenda and sensitive notes

Added by the expansion plan's step 10 (Tahseen Arnan). Nothing above changes: a signed note is still locked.

- **Add an addendum.** On a signed note, **Add an addendum** opens a page with one text field and the note under it. **Sign addendum** signs the text as you, with the time. The note's page lists its addenda under the note, oldest first, each with its author and time. An addendum can't be edited or removed; write another.
- **Who.** Any clinician with the patient on their panel, not only the note's signer. Under emergency access the note and its addenda can be read, and the link isn't offered.
- **An addendum by someone else** (step 10b) is labeled "Addendum by *name*, not the note's author", on the note's page and in the desktop's offline copy, and the note's signer gets an inbox item for it: "An addendum was added to a note you signed", with the patient and a link to the note, and none of its text. The signer marks it done from the inbox. A signer who can no longer sign in is not told.
- **Sensitive notes.** On the confirmation page, tick **Sensitive** before signing. A sensitive note follows the rules for sensitive items (UC 4): the chart leaves it out and says how many notes are hidden until someone chooses **Show sensitive items**; each view is recorded as a view of sensitive items; an administrator's audit log shows that it was written and never its text; and it is never kept in the desktop app's offline copy.
- **Marking sensitive after signing** (step 10b). On a signed note, **Mark this note sensitive** asks for a reason and marks it; each addendum has its own **Mark sensitive** link. The text never changes. The note shows who marked it, when and why ("Marked sensitive by *name*"), and the change is in the audit log. The form posts the change it offered: sent twice, or after someone else has already made the change, it changes nothing and says so.
  - Any clinician with the patient on their panel can mark. **Only the person who marked it, or the note's signer, can unmark it**, also with a reason; anyone else is told so and offered no form. A note signed as sensitive counts as marked by its signer, and an addendum written as sensitive as marked by its author.
  - Marking a note marks each of its addenda that isn't marked already, recorded as inherited from the note and shown as "Sensitive with the note". An addendum signed to a sensitive note is inherited too.
  - Unmarking the note clears the inherited marks, with the same reason, and only those. An addendum marked on its own, at signing or afterwards, stays marked until it is unmarked itself. No addendum can be unmarked while its note is sensitive.
  - Views of it made before it was marked are already in the audit log and stay there. From then on, an administrator gets none of its text from the log, including what was saved while it was a draft.
  - The desktop's draft sync is told: a desktop that has the note's draft text empties it at its next sync of that patient, and the next offline copy leaves the note out. A desktop syncs the patients on its clinician's panel: if the patient has left the panel, its copy of the draft stays until the patient is back on it.
  - An addendum signed at the moment its note is being marked ends sensitive: the two wait for each other.
- **Sensitive addenda.** An addendum to a sensitive note is sensitive. On another note, tick **Sensitive** on the addendum: the note's page leaves it out, says how many are hidden, and **Show sensitive addenda** shows them and records the view.
- **Off-site.** The note and its addenda are read from the cloud copy and signing is forwarded to the clinic's server. While that server can't be reached, the link isn't offered.
- **Desktop app.** The offline chart has a **Signed notes** section: each of a patient's ten latest signed notes that isn't sensitive, with its addenda. It is read-only. "Sensitive items not kept offline" counts sensitive notes too, and a note says how many sensitive addenda it has that aren't in the copy.
- **A sensitive note's draft on the desktop.** The draft sync (UC 8) sends the draft of a note signed as sensitive with no text, marked withheld. A desktop that had the draft's text empties its copy, whatever state it was in, and shows "Signed as a sensitive note · Not kept on this device". A desktop build from before this step doesn't know the mark: it replaces a synced copy with the empty text, but keeps text that was typed on it and never sent.
- **Audit log.** When a note is signed as sensitive, the audit events of its draft, and of its dictation reviews and their proposed fields, are marked sensitive with it: they hold the same text.
- **Notes don't reach the portal.** Neither a note nor an addendum is shown there, to the patient or a proxy. What reaches it is a visit summary written for the patient (below, step 13a), which follows `ehr.portal_sees_sensitive()` as other sensitive items do (UC 18).

## Visit summaries for the patient

Added by the expansion plan's step 13a (Tahseen Arnan). A visit summary is what the patient reads of a visit: written for them, from a signed note, and never the note itself. Nothing above changes: the note and its addenda stay out of the portal.

- **Write one.** On a signed note, under its addenda, **Write a summary for the patient** opens a page with three fields, each required and up to 4,000 characters: **What we talked about**, **What we found** and **What happens next**. The cursor starts in the first. The note is under the form, for reference: nothing of it is copied in, so the clinician writes the summary in their own words rather than release the care team's shorthand by default. **Release to the patient** releases it, as you, with the time. The patient reads it in the portal as written, line breaks kept; so does anyone they share their record with (UC 6).
- **Who.** Any clinician with the patient on their panel, not only the note's signer: the note's page says who released it ("Released to the patient by *name*", on the clinic's clock). Under emergency access the summary is read and nothing is offered.
- **One at a time.** A note has at most one summary standing in the portal. A released summary is never edited: a wrong one is withdrawn, and a new one written.
- **Withdraw one.** **Withdraw this summary…** asks for a reason (required, up to 500 characters). Withdrawn, its text is no longer shown in the portal, to the patient or anyone else: the portal keeps a placeholder where it was, with its date and nothing else (UC 6). It stays on the note's page, struck through, with "Withdrawn by *name*", the time and the reason, and views of it before then stay in the audit log. Any clinician with the patient on their panel may withdraw one. Tahseen's decisions of 21 November 2026:
  - If the patient or a proxy had viewed it in the portal, the patient is emailed as for a new summary (below), whatever was sent that day. "Viewed" means the portal recorded a read of that summary, which it does for each summary its page shows, by the patient's account or a proxy's; a clinician's reading doesn't count. The database answers it only for the summary the clinician's own transaction has just withdrawn (`ehr.withdrawn_summary_was_viewed`, which checks that this transaction wrote the summary's row): clinicians can't read the audit log. Its limits: a clinician could withdraw a summary, ask and roll back, which leaves no trace; and a view is inferred from audit rows that SQL run as the app's database role, or the cloud app's shipper, can insert. The most either can do is send the patient one generic email. A view made off-site reaches the clinic's audit log when the cloud app ships it, within seconds while the link to the clinic is up; while the link is down the view waits in the cloud app until it is back, so a summary withdrawn in between counts as not viewed and no email is sent.
  - If someone other than the clinician who released it withdraws it, the releaser gets an inbox item, "A visit summary you released was withdrawn", with the patient and a link to the note, and none of the summary or the reason, as for another clinician's addendum. A releaser who can no longer sign in is not told.
- **What the form refuses.** A summary is not released while it has, in any part: an internal identifier (a UUID); the patient's own medical record number; a diagnosis code (ICD-10-CM, with its dot or without: "E11.9", "E119", "I10"), a medication code (RxNorm, 5 to 7 digits standing alone), a lab test code (LOINC, "4548-4"), a substance code (UNII) or one of the clinic's own order codes ("SYN-LAB-001"), each only when it is a real code in the reference data; or a whole section of the note (Subjective, Objective, Assessment or Plan, or the History of a note signed before step 14, when it is 40 characters or more) copied in, whatever its case and spacing, read as it looks, as below. The page says what it found and where ("Not released: “E11.9” in What we found is a diagnosis code. Say what it means in plain words.") and keeps what was typed. Only the parts it names are marked invalid, each with its messages as its description, so a screen reader reads them at the part. Ordinary words, doses, dates and numbers pass: "B12", "A1c", "N95", "500 mg", "50,000 units", "2026-11-21", "1-2 tablets". The database holds the same rule, in one place (`ehr.visit_summary_problems`), and checks it as the summary is released, before it takes the note's lock; the page asks it again only to say what it found when it refuses one. Each word shaped like a code is looked up once, by index, so a summary full of words like "B12" is released about as quickly as one without.
- **Look-alikes** are read as they look: the rule takes out characters that show nothing (zero-width spaces and joiners, the soft hyphen, direction marks), reads fullwidth letters and digits and other compatibility forms as the plain ones, and every common hyphen and dash (the en and em dashes, the minus sign) as "-". It says what it found as it read it ("E11.9" for "Ｅ１１．９"); what is released is what the clinician typed.
- **The rule's known limit.** It can't stop a clinician pasting a fragment of the note, typing a code in another form (with a space in it, say, even a hair space that is nearly invisible, or with a letter of another alphabet that looks like a Latin one, Cyrillic "Е" for "E"), writing a category that isn't itself a code ("E11"), or naming another patient's record number; and RxNorm codes of four digits or fewer aren't looked for, since they can't be told from years and doses (RxNorm's 1998 is captopril). The summary is the clinician's own writing and the clinician's own release.
- **Sensitive** (Tahseen's decision of 21 November 2026). A summary starts sensitive if its note or any of its addenda is sensitive when it is released (inherited, and the form says so rather than offer the tick), or if the clinician ticks **Sensitive** as they release it (a mark of its own). Afterwards it follows the rules for addenda: when the note or any addendum of it becomes sensitive (marked, or an addendum signed sensitive), the summary becomes sensitive in the same statement, recorded as inherited ("Sensitive with the note"); the inherited mark is cleared when none of them is sensitive any more. Both carry a reason of the database's ("Its note or an addendum to the note is sensitive.", "Neither its note nor any addendum to the note is sensitive now."), never the words given for the note or the addendum, which stay on that row. **Mark sensitive** on a summary marks it on its own, with a reason; only the person who marked it, or the note's signer, unmarks it, also with a reason, and no summary can be unmarked while the note or an addendum is sensitive. Withdrawn summaries follow the same marks. The database does it all. A sensitive summary of a note that isn't sensitive is left out of the note's page until someone asks ("Show sensitive summaries"), and that view is recorded; once sensitive, the audit log gives an administrator none of its text. In the portal a sensitive summary is shown to the patient when they ask for it, and to a proxy only when the patient allows them sensitive items (UC 6, UC 18).
- **Telling the patient.** When a summary is released, the patient's own portal account gets an email: "There is something new in your patient portal", with a button to the portal's visit summaries, built from the portal's configured address, and nothing about the summary, the visit or the clinician. At most one a day for a patient: none when another summary was released for them earlier the same day, the clinic's day from midnight in its time zone. The same email when a summary they or a proxy had viewed is withdrawn, which the one-a-day rule doesn't hold back. Never to a proxy, as for messages.
- **The chart** tags a signed note that has a summary standing with "Summary released". It is asked in the chart's existing notes query: no statement of its own.
- **Off-site.** The note's summaries are read from the cloud copy, and releasing and withdrawing are forwarded to the clinic's server. While that server can't be reached neither is offered: "A summary can't be released until the clinic's server is back."
- **Desktop app.** The offline copy doesn't carry summaries: it already holds the signed notes they are written from.
- **Not built:** a FHIR resource for a summary (UC 19 serves none).

## Four sections: Subjective, Objective, Assessment and Plan

Added by the expansion plan's step 14 (Tahseen Arnan). A note is written in four sections, each required before signing, as the three were:

- **Subjective:** what the patient reports: the reason for the visit, the symptoms and their timing, the relevant past and family history, medications and allergies, and readings they took at home.
- **Objective:** what the clinician found: today's vital signs and examination findings, and the results the clinician has already reviewed.
- **Assessment** and **Plan** as before.

A section that shows nothing isn't signed: nothing visible is left once whitespace of every kind is taken out, and the characters that show nothing which step 13a's summary rule strips (zero-width spaces, joiners, direction marks and the like). "Sign note…" asks for it as for an empty one ("Enter Objective before signing."), and the database refuses it too. The draft keeps what was typed.

The editor says under each section's heading what it holds: those words for Subjective and Objective, and the dictation note schema's own descriptions for Assessment and Plan.

**Visit types.** Above the sections, **Visit type** lists None and the visit types the clinic keeps as reference data (`db/reference/37-note-templates.sql`: follow-up, new patient, acute, well-child and telephone visits). Choosing one shows, under each section, what to cover in it for that kind of visit ("Follow-up visit: How each problem has been since the last visit: ..."), and saves the choice with the draft, as a typed change is saved. The prompts are guidance: nothing of them is put into the note, and the signed note doesn't keep the visit type. A visit type that isn't on the list isn't saved ("Choose a visit type from the list."). The prompts were written for this project and are not validated for clinical use. Appointments and visits have no type: the clinician chooses one for the note.

**Vital signs for Objective.** Under Objective, **Vital signs for Objective…** (shown by the editor's script, so not without JavaScript) asks for the patient's vital signs, and only then are they read, as the vital signs page reads them, recorded with that part ("Vital signs" in the patient's "Who viewed my record"). They are those of the latest day on the clinic's calendar on which a vital sign was taken that stands: the newest of each sign that day, a blood pressure as one reading, none entered in error, each with its time on the clinic's clock, labeled, and the time once when they share it: "Vital signs, 2026-11-22 10:41 CST: blood pressure 126/80 mmHg, heart rate 72 per minute, temperature 37.9 °C." When that day isn't today the offer says so first ("Not today: these were taken on 2026-11-19."), and with none, "None recorded: this patient has no vital signs to add." Nothing is added until the clinician chooses **Add to Objective**, which puts the text at the end of Objective, after a blank line, moves the cursor there and says "Vital signs added to Objective. Check them before signing."; it is then saved as typing is, and can be changed like anything typed. The note keeps only that text. A value taken in the hour the clocks repeat when they go back is on that day either way, and its label (CDT or CST) says which hour. Only for a patient on the clinician's panel and the clinician's own unsigned draft of that patient (asked for with another draft, or once the patient has left the panel, it is not found, and nothing is read); off-site they are read from the cloud copy, and while the clinic's server can't be reached the editor, and with it the offer, isn't shown. A note isn't tied to a visit, so the offer is the latest day's rather than a chosen visit's.

Each section holds up to 16,000 characters, and the note 64 KiB, as before. The limit counts the draft's plain text, headings included, and the four headings take 15 bytes more than History, Assessment and Plan did, and 43 more than the desktop app's plain text, which has none: a draft written before step 14, or last saved on the desktop, whose text is within those few bytes of the limit isn't saved in four sections as it stands, and the editor says "This draft is at the size limit: shorten it by a few words to save it in four sections." The limit wasn't raised for it. The draft's plain text, which the desktop app keeps (UC 8), has the four headings, each on its own line before its text; the desktop app's drafts are otherwise unchanged.

**Notes and drafts written before step 14** are kept as they were written: nothing was moved or rewritten, since what was signed under History held today's vital signs, examination findings and results too, and can't be split into Subjective and Objective without changing it.

- A signed note keeps its History, Assessment and Plan, and every page shows it under those headings, with "Written as History, Assessment and Plan, before notes had four sections." Its sensitivity changes as any note's does; its text never does.
- An unsigned draft opens in the editor with its History in Subjective and a notice: "Check: this draft was written before notes had four sections. Its History is now in Subjective: move today's vital signs, examination findings and the results you reviewed to Objective before signing." Opening it changes nothing; the draft keeps its History until its author saves it, and that save writes the four sections and clears the History in one update. The database signs no draft that still has a History, and no note with one. Shown read-only (off-site with the clinic's server down, on the confirmation and in a review's "Current saved note"), it is shown as saved, under History, Assessment and Plan.
- A draft last saved by the desktop app opens with all its text in Subjective, as it opened in History before, with a notice to sort it into the four sections.
- Dictation sorts into the same four sections (UC 10, UC 13). A review of a dictation sorted before step 14 that is still waiting shows its History, Assessment and Plan, labels the History "History (goes into Subjective)", and puts an approved History into Subjective, after any text there when appending is allowed (UC 13); the database stages no new History proposal.
- **Desktop app.** The offline copy carries a note signed in four sections with its Subjective and Objective, which the desktop app shows under those headings, and gives them under History too, each under its heading, so a desktop build from before step 14, which knows only History, Assessment and Plan, shows all of it. A note signed before comes as it was signed. The desktop app's drafts are unchanged: their plain text has the four headings when the web saved them last.

`db/policies/50-clinical-notes.sql`'s signing trigger (`ehr.finalize_clinical_note`) checks the four sections against the saved draft, refuses a History, and refuses a section that shows nothing (`ehr.note_section_is_blank`, the app's `NoteContent.IsBlank`); `52-visit-summaries.sql`'s rule compares a summary with whichever sections the note has; `10-grants.sql` lets both apps read the visit-type templates (`db/reference/37-note-templates.sql`). ## Persistence, concurrency and security review

The EF migration adds nullable `history`, `assessment`, `plan`, `structured_version`, and nonnullable `has_unreviewed_proposals` (default false) to UC 8's existing `clinical_drafts`. Old drafts and sync creates remain valid. `Body` is still plain text, never JSON. A web save renders the structured sections into plain text for legacy desktop readers and updates both `Version` and `StructuredVersion`. Desktop writes still modify only Body and Version. A version mismatch makes the old structured fields ineligible for signing; reopening imports current Body into History and asks the clinician to check/complete all three fields and save.

`clinical_notes` is the single immutable signed-record store: ID, unique DraftId, PatientId, signed DraftVersion, History, Assessment, Plan, SignerId and SignedAt (since step 14, Subjective and Objective in place of History; above). Foreign keys preserve patient, draft and staff relationships; the chart has a patient/time index. The source draft remains frozen for existing sync clients. There are no duplicate mutable note records and no delete/tombstone change to the draft-sync contract.

The page transaction filter commits the signed record, source-draft version advance, change log, trigger write audit and `sign_note` event together. The database BEFORE INSERT trigger locks the draft with FOR UPDATE, verifies patient, exact version and structured snapshot, rejects incomplete/oversized fields or pending AI review, assigns signer and timestamp itself, and advances the draft's sync version. The unique DraftId constraint and row lock prevent duplicate final records. A repeated completed signature returns the original note; competing signatures or saves may return 409 and can be reconciled against the chart. A draft UPDATE guard prevents changes after signing, including desktop sync. Already-applied desktop operation keys retain the original duplicate receipt outcome; new edit keys are refused after signing. A signed-note immutability trigger and absence of UPDATE/DELETE grants prevent overwriting finalized content.

All note PHI reads go through `PhiReader` and are audited. Architecture checks now include drafts and signed notes. Both PHI tables have forced RLS and standard write-audit triggers. Signed-note INSERT is limited to a clinician signing as themselves, with the invoker signing trigger requiring their own visible active-panel draft. Draft RLS remains UC 8's private-author/panel policy. Cloud reads get SELECT on drafts under the same RLS; cloud notes are SELECT-only. The cloud audit whitelist is extended narrowly to row IDs from `clinical_drafts` and `clinical_notes`, with no reason or details; its normal durable queue and authenticated import remain in use. Cloud POSTs still forward to on-prem. `NoteWriteAuthority` also refuses domain writes on a cloud-configured host; PostgreSQL replica/read-only/grant enforcement remains a separate boundary.

Human line-by-line review is required for `db/policies` and the touched draft-sync conflict handling under AGENTS.md. Review especially the draft row lock, signed snapshot/version comparison, draft freeze, signer/time assignment, RLS/grants and cloud audit shapes. No RLS bypass or new SECURITY DEFINER function is introduced. No note content is written to application logs. The established database audit retains PHI changes as before.

## UML update

- `ClinicalDraft` gains optional History, Assessment, Plan and StructuredVersion, plus server-owned HasUnreviewedProposals. Its existing ID, PatientId, ClinicianId, Body and Version remain.
- `ClinicalNote` stores the immutable snapshot, DraftVersion, SignerId and SignedAt. Each draft has zero or one signed note; each patient and staff clinician can have many drafts/notes. ClinicalNote references ClinicalDraft, Patient and StaffUser.
- `ClinicalNotes` depends on EhrDbContext, PhiReader, UserContext, AuditRecorder and NoteWriteAuthority. Public operations: CreateAsync, SaveAsync, SignAsync and SigningProblems. NoteResult returns status, errors and an optional draft/final note.
- `NoteContent` carries History/Assessment/Plan, supplies Body, From(draft) and Validate(signing).
- `PhiReader` gains NoteDraftsAsync, NotesAsync, SignedDraftAsync and NoteAsync. Its private ReadNoteAsync centralizes signed-note read auditing.
- ChartModel uses ClinicalNotes for creation and PhiReader for lists. EditModel uses the domain save operation; ConfirmModel validates and calls SignAsync; ViewModel displays a final note. All three inherit PatientNotePage's chart access/identity loader. NoteWriteExceptionFilter translates rolled-back version/guard conflicts into 409 responses.
