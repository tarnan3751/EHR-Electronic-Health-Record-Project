# UC 18 — Give someone proxy access to a record

SwiftChart EHR · the expansion plan's step 6 (FR-19) · Tahseen Arnan · a proposed use case, not in the course document

## How it works

- **A proxy** is someone who uses the portal for a patient: a parent for a child, a carer for a relative. They sign in with their own portal account and their own password, never the patient's. They don't have to be a patient of the clinic.
- **Someone with no portal account** gets one from the email that tells them access was given (step 6b). Its button, **Create your portal account**, opens a page that asks for their name and a password. The link works once and expires after 7 days. The account uses the address the email was sent to, and has no record of its own: it shows Access and the records shared with it, and nothing else.
- **After signing in**, an account with records shared with it is asked **Whose record?**: "My record" first, when it has one, then each shared record. An account with nothing shared goes straight to its own record.
- **The patient gives access** in the portal, under **Access**: the other person's portal email, what they can see, and for how long. The patient can let them see items the care team marked sensitive, change that, and remove the access at any time.
- **The clinic records a proxy** from the chart's **Proxy access** section, for a patient on the clinician's panel. A proxy the clinic records never sees sensitive items; only the patient can turn that on.
- **The proxy chooses a record** after signing in, and can change it at any time under Access, in **Records you can view**. Every page then shows a band, "Viewing as a proxy: this is … 's record, not your own.", with **Back to my own record**. That part of the flow is UC 6's (`docs/UC6.md`).
- **What they can see** is one of two scopes: the record, results and appointments; or those and messages. With messages, a proxy can write to the care team for the patient, and the clinician sees the message as from "Name (proxy)".
- **The proxy is told by email** that someone gave them access: where to sign in, and the button that creates an account for someone who has none. The email names nobody.

## Decisions

- **Data.** `ehr.proxy_grants`: the patient, the proxy's portal email, the scope, whether sensitive items are included, who gave it (the patient or staff) and when, when it expires, and when and by whom it was revoked. Patient data: row-level security is enabled and forced, and the write-audit trigger records every write. Never deleted.
- **A grant is addressed to an email**, not to an account. It works for the confirmed portal account with that address, and the patient's list never says whether one exists, so the form can't be used to find out who has an account.
- **Proxy-only accounts** (step 6b). A portal account's patient is optional (`patient_users.patient_id` may be null). Such an account has no patient claim in its session; the portal's policy accepts any portal account, and `PortalSubjectFilter` sends a session with no record to look at to "Whose record?" before any other page's handler runs. In the database it is nobody's patient (`ehr.own_patient_id()` is null), so by itself it reads no row of any patient table and can share nothing.
- **Invitations** (`ehr.proxy_invitations`): the grant, the address, the hash of a code, when it expires and when it was used. Internal, like enrollment codes, because the page that accepts one runs before anyone is signed in. Opening the emailed link shows the address is theirs, so the account is created with it already confirmed. An invitation opens nothing by itself: what the account sees is whatever grants are addressed to its email. An unknown, used or expired code gets one answer; an address that already has a confirmed account is told to sign in. An account that named the address without ever confirming it gives the address up to whoever accepts the invitation, since they have shown the mailbox is theirs; its patient registers again with a new enrollment code. The code is claimed in one statement, so two posts of the same link create one account. The page is limited per client address like registration.
- **The session only names the grant.** A cookie holds the grant's ID; the app passes it to the database beside the signed-in account (`app.acting_grant`). `ehr.current_patient_id()`, which every patient-side policy uses, answers with the grant's patient only while the grant is addressed to this account, not revoked and not expired. Otherwise it answers nobody, never the account's own patient, so an ended grant shows nothing rather than the wrong person's record.
- **One record at a time.** While looking at someone else's record a session doesn't see its own, or any other record shared with it, and the other way round. Before choosing, a proxy is given the names of the patients who shared a record and nothing else of theirs, by a database function that answers only for the caller's own address (`ehr.proxied_records`).
- **Plain ASCII addresses only**, for the grant and for the account that uses it, and only the characters a portal account's address may have (letters, digits and `-._@+`). Identity's email normalizing folds some look-alike letters together, and a grant must not match a look-alike address.
- **Sensitive items** (step 2: diagnoses and medications marked sensitive) are held back from a proxy by row-level security unless the grant includes them, and only the patient, as themselves, can change that; a trigger refuses it from staff and from the proxy. The change is in the write audit under the patient's account.
- **Messages** are held back from a proxy by row-level security unless the grant's scope includes them. A proxy's reading never marks the care team's messages read: the patient still finds them new. What a proxy sends counts toward the patient's limits (10 new conversations a day, 30 messages an hour).
- **A proxy never passes access on.** Giving, changing and removing access is for the patient as themselves, or a clinician with the patient on their panel.
- **At most 20 in force** that the patient gave; expired ones and the clinic's don't count.
- **Always an expiry**, at most a year away. The forms offer 30 days, 90 days, 6 months and 1 year.
- **Giving it again replaces it**: one grant in force per patient and proxy.
- **The audit names both.** Everything a proxy reads or writes is recorded with the proxy's account as the actor and the patient as the subject.
- **Portal pages take their patient from `PortalSubject`**, never from the request and no longer from the sign-in claim. `PortalSubjectTests` holds them to that, and allows a grant's ID in a request, never a patient's.
- **Off-site.** Lists are read from the cloud copy; giving and removing is forwarded to the clinic's server, and replaced by a sentence while it can't be reached. Choosing a record saves nothing (it is a cookie), so the cloud app handles it itself and it works during an outage.
- **Ended access.** When a grant ends while a proxy is looking at the record, their next page is their own record, with "Access ended: you can no longer view that record. This is your own."

## Limits

- An invitation isn't cancelled when the access it was sent for is removed: the account can still be created, and sees nothing.
- A proxy-only account can't be turned into a patient's account if that person later becomes a patient; they would register with their enrollment code under another address.
- The clinic has no page that lists proxy-only accounts or disables one; removing the access is what stops it seeing a record.
- The portal doesn't show diagnoses or medications yet, so the sensitive-item rule is enforced and tested in the database with nothing on a portal page to show for it. It will matter as soon as those lists, or sensitive notes (step 10), reach the portal.
- Results and appointments have no sensitive mark: a proxy sees all of them.
- Two scopes only. No access by age, and no automatic end when a child becomes an adult.
- The reply notice for a message goes to the patient's own email, not the proxy's.
- Switching records is not itself an audit event; what is then read is.
- Off-site, the cloud copy lags the clinic's server by a moment, so a removed proxy can keep reading there until it catches up.
- A portal account whose email has characters outside plain ASCII can't be a proxy.
- The desktop app has no portal, so nothing changes there.
