# UC 24 — Review an emergency access

SwiftChart EHR · the expansion plan's step 18 (proposed FR-64) · Tahseen Arnan · a proposed use case, not in the course document

## Administrator workflow

- **An access arrives.** When a clinician opens a chart off their panel through emergency access (UC 12), the access
  appears in the inbox of every administrator whose account isn't disabled, except the clinician who used it, who may
  be an administrator too. The item says only "A clinician used emergency access to a chart", with the patient by ID
  and **Review the access**. It has no **Mark done**: it is done by reviewing.
- **Review.** The access's page (`/admin/emergency-access/{id}`) shows the clinician by name and email, the patient by
  ID, when the access began and ended (UTC, as the audit log), the reason given, and what the clinician did to that
  patient while it lasted, from the audit log: each event's time, what, which part of the record, which row, and from
  which site, the first 100 with a link to the rest on the audit page. Never a value from the record: no event's
  details, no word of a note, message or order.
- **Once it has ended.** While the access is in force the page says "In force until …", with the time, near the top, and offers nothing to do: the review
  covers everything done under it. A clinician's emergency access lasts four hours.
- **Mark reviewed.** Say whether it was appropriate ("Appropriate" or "Not appropriate", required) and write a note of
  10 to 500 characters, then **Mark reviewed**: "Reviewed.". The review shows the outcome, who reviewed it and when, and
  the note. Every administrator's item for the access is done. The clinician doesn't see the review.
- **The audit page.** Each emergency access is tinted and tagged **Review** until reviewed, then **Reviewed**, and
  **Not appropriate** when it was found so, each with **Open the review**. **Review** filters the page to the accesses
  waiting for review, or to those reviewed as not appropriate.
- **What follows "Not appropriate"** (telling the clinician, or the patient) is not built: the expansion plan lists it
  for later.

## Decisions

- **Data.** `ehr.emergency_access_reviews`: keyed by the access's ID, so one review an access; who reviewed it and when,
  both set by the database, the outcome and the note. Restricted, like the audit log it belongs with: row-level
  security is enabled and forced, administrators read every review and add one as themselves, nobody else reads one,
  and nothing changes or deletes one, the tables' owner included (`db/policies/72-emergency-access-review.sql`). No
  write-audit trigger: the row is its own record. A partial index of the audit log's emergency accesses
  (`ix_audit_events_break_glass`) lets the audit page's filters for them read those rows alone.
- **Administrators still read no patient data.** They have no policy on `ehr.break_glass_accesses`; the page gets an
  access's patient, clinician, reason and times from `ehr.emergency_access_for_review`, which runs as the tables' owner
  and answers an administrator alone, for the one access asked for. The break_glass audit event already shows them all
  but the end. Nothing read here is a recorded read of the patient.
- **Who reviews.** An administrator who isn't the access's clinician, once the access has ended, by the database's
  clock. The database refuses anyone else, a review before the end, one's own, a second review, and a note outside 10 to
  500 characters once trimmed; the page says which.
- **The inbox.** A trigger that runs as the tables' owner puts each new access in every administrator's inbox but its
  clinician's and the disabled (locked out for good; one locked for a while after failed sign-ins counts), and a
  review marks every item for it done; no runtime role can mark one done (`70-inbox.sql`). An item of the kind with no
  access behind it is marked done as any other.
- **Never in the way of emergency access.** The delivery can't refuse the access: with no administrator, nobody is told;
  if writing the items fails, the failure is swallowed, an audit event `review_items_failed` records the access, the
  clinician and the error's code and nothing else, the server's log says the same, and the migration job makes up the
  miss.
- **The migration job's sweep.** On every run, each access not yet reviewed goes to each administrator who could review
  it and hasn't an item for it yet: the accesses from before this step, administrators added since, and any delivery
  that failed. It locks the reviews' table while it runs, so a review added meanwhile can't leave an item no review
  closes.
- **The reason stays visible to the reviewer,** after step 18a too (Tahseen's answer of 24 November 2026): it is the
  clinician's statement, written for this review.
- **Off-site.** The page is read from the cloud copy; **Mark reviewed** is forwarded to the clinic's server. While that
  can't be reached the form is replaced by "Marking an access reviewed needs the clinic's server.", and the "Not saved" page shows a note typed as the link dropped.

|---|---|---|
