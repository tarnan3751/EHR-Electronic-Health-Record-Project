# UC 10 — Structure and validate note fields

SwiftChart EHR · Samir Mukshe · CS 3354 Software Engineering, Fall 2026

Once a dictation stops, the sidecar asks the language model (K2 Horizon, through llama-server) to sort
the transcript into the note's fields, and keeps only values grounded in what was said (`validation.py`). The fields
come back awaiting the clinician's review (UC 13).

Since the expansion plan's step 15 (FR-14 as reworded), the result also proposes, once the transcript is final, each
order or allergy to add the clinician dictated in a closed sentence, from the instructions it already lists as not carried over:
its kind, where it was said and its warnings, never words, and never anything the model wrote (`validation.proposals()`,
which a kind of session that must propose nothing switches off with `Session.proposes`). A closed sentence is one in
which every word is accounted for: a closed lead-in ("go ahead and", "please", "I'll"...), the verb in one of its plain
forms, determiners, an item the reference data names (`catalog.py`, generated from `db/reference` and the clinic's common
catalog items: an orderable for an order, an allergen for an allergy to add), the item's quantities and a closed set of
trailers, and nothing else (`validation.py`'s header has the classes word for word). Any other word, anything the
reference data doesn't name, an unclear passage, or anything but an order or an allergy added, holds it back, and it
stays listed as not carried over; the sentences next to it are read only for a closed list of cues (a retraction, doubt
or refusal after it, a report or a list of what not to do before it). A proposal can still be wrong, and is the
clinician's to dismiss. The review shows each proposal for the clinician to open in the order form or the allergy form,
or dismiss (UC 13, UC 1, UC 4). The eval measures them, against two golds as it does the fields: what the rules allow (each
script's own proposals) and every order or allergy to add a clinician would expect proposed (the clinical gold); its
gate requires the owner to accept every false or misheard proposal (`eval/README.md`).

Since the expansion plan's step 17 (proposed FR-61), a dictation in Spanish is sorted with a Spanish prompt and note
schema into the same four fields in English: one list of statements, each its Spanish passages, its field and its
English. `translation.py` checks each statement against the Spanish it cites, class by class and in the order the
Spanish says them, refusing what it can't compare: numbers with their units and what each measures, how long, how
often, dates, drugs, what is done with each drug and habit and when, sides, routes, dose forms, severity,
abbreviations, what can be ordered, opposites, findings that may never be added, negation, certainty, history and
relatives; one statement failing discards its field. It doesn't check the meaning of other words: the clinician
compares each sentence with the Spanish shown beside it. The instructions its detectors find, said in Spanish or in
English, and the Spanish of any statement whose English is one, are cut and listed as not carried over, and nothing
is proposed from Spanish. The result says it was in Spanish, gives each field's statements, and names Whisper.

## Limits

- TC-UC10-19's eval half checks the visits' committed results the same way, on synthetic voices only.
- TC-UC10-06 checks the committed eval results, made on a developer Mac, not a run on every change: CI can't run the
  models. The gate has no accepted baseline yet, so "no lower than the baseline" has nothing to compare with until one
  is accepted.
- TC-UC10-18 checks `eval/results/latest-es.json`, which the Spanish eval on audio hasn't written yet: the gate fails
  until it is committed. The checks compare the kinds of word that carry most risk, in order, not the meaning of every
  word: a word left untranslated that the transcript says, or written in English's letters, a finding the two
  languages spell differently moved to another severity or qualifier, the gender of "su", a false friend
  ("constipado", "embarazada", "intoxicado", "sensible", "molestia") and a condition dropped pass them
  (`translation.py`'s header). The clinician's reading of each sentence beside its Spanish is the check for those.
  An instruction the detectors miss, translated as a plain statement, reaches the note.
